# Lovevery for International Families: Which Site to Order From, and What This Guide's US-Focused Math Doesn't Cover

**Bottom line up front:** Lovevery operates through four separate regional storefronts, not one global site, and where you actually order from depends entirely on where you live. More importantly for anyone who's read the rest of this content series: the resale-value math that runs through several of our other guides — the official Pre-Loved marketplace's brand-reported 70% average resale value — is confirmed contiguous-US-only. If you're ordering from outside the US, a meaningful part of this cluster's financial case doesn't directly apply to you, and this guide covers what changes as a result.

## The Four Official Storefronts

Lovevery doesn't run one international site with automatic regional detection — it operates four separate storefronts, each serving a defined region:

| Storefront | Serves | Shipping Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Lovevery.com | United States, Canada, and APO/FPO/DPO military addresses | United States |
| Lovevery.eu | European Union countries | Germany |
| Lovevery.co.uk | United Kingdom | United Kingdom |
| Lovevery.com.au | Australia, New Zealand, Singapore | Not confirmed in this research — check the site directly |

This split is confirmed directly through Lovevery's own help documentation across multiple regional support sites, and it's clearly a common point of confusion — it shows up as a standalone FAQ entry on several of the company's own regional help pages. If you're ordering from within one of these covered regions, the practical step is straightforward: use the storefront for your region rather than defaulting to the US site out of habit or because it's the most commonly referenced one online (including, likely, most of the content in this cluster, which is written with the US market as its primary frame).

**One thing we couldn't confirm:** whether the box lineup, naming (The Looker, The Charmer, and so on), and stage timing are identical across all four storefronts, or whether any region carries a modified catalog. If you're outside the US, it's worth checking your regional site's current lineup directly against the box-by-box detail in our [core audit](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) rather than assuming full parity — most of the developmental logic should transfer regardless of minor catalog differences, but specific item names or availability may not match exactly.

## If You're Outside All Four Regions

A meaningful part of the world — much of Asia outside Singapore, Africa, South America, most of the Middle East, and large parts of Eastern Europe — isn't covered by any of the four official storefronts. The only route into the product from those locations is an unofficial freight-forwarding service: a company that provides you a US or UK shipping address, receives the package on your behalf, and forwards it internationally.

**This works, but it comes with a real, confirmed tradeoff worth understanding before you rely on it.** Per Lovevery's own stated policy, once an order is delivered to any address — including a freight forwarder's address — the company's responsibility for that order ends. If the package is then lost, damaged, or goes missing during the forwarder's leg of the journey, Lovevery is explicitly unable to replace, reship, or refund it. Returns and cancellations are also unavailable once an order has shipped to a third-party forwarding address. This isn't a freight-forwarder-specific policy quirk — it's a standard limitation of using any forwarding service with any retailer, but it's worth being explicit about here, since it means you're taking on real risk that a domestic or officially-supported-region buyer doesn't have to weigh.

**Practical implication:** if you're using a freight forwarder, treat the transaction as higher-risk than a standard order — budget for the possibility of a lost or damaged shipment without recourse, and weigh that risk against how much you want the product specifically versus a locally-available alternative, including the competitor brands covered in our [competitor comparison guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/beyond-lovevery-monti-kids-hoppi-box-kiwico-panda-crates), some of which may have better-established distribution in your region.

## The Single Biggest Correction for International Readers of This Content Series

This is worth stating plainly rather than burying in a caveat, because it changes a real conclusion elsewhere in this cluster: our [resale valuation guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-vs-amazon-diy-montessori-12-month-experiment) and several other articles build meaningful parts of their financial case on Lovevery's official Pre-Loved marketplace, which carries a brand-reported average of 70% resale value retained — a genuinely strong figure that pulls the effective cost of the subscription down substantially in our net-cost calculations. **That marketplace is confirmed contiguous-US-only.**

If you're ordering from Canada, the UK, the EU, Australia, or anywhere else, that specific resale channel isn't available to you. Your realistic resale options are the informal channels our own research found running meaningfully lower — the 30-60% range for individual items sold through general marketplaces like local Facebook groups, eBay in your region, or equivalent local platforms, rather than the platform-graded 70% average. Concretely: if you're using the net-effective-cost figures from our [core cost guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) or [Amazon DIY comparison](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-vs-amazon-diy-montessori-12-month-experiment) to budget, and you're ordering internationally, you should generally use the lower, informal-channel end of those ranges rather than the Pre-Loved-based figures, since the stronger resale channel simply isn't accessible to you.

This doesn't mean the subscription is a worse value internationally in every respect — the pass-down and durability mechanics covered throughout this content series (which don't depend on any specific resale platform) apply the same way regardless of where you live. It specifically affects the resale-recovery side of the math, not the pass-down side.

## Does the Sibling Pass-Down Math Still Work the Same Way?

Largely yes, with the caveat above about resale specifically. The core mechanic our [core cost guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) and [box-by-box audit](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) describe — a child aging out of a stage right around when a younger sibling is ready for it — is a function of child development timing and physical object durability, neither of which is region-specific. If your regional storefront carries a comparable lineup (which, per the caveat above, we'd recommend confirming rather than assuming), the pass-down savings mechanism should work essentially the same way for an international household as for a US one, minus the Pre-Loved resale boost specifically.

## Registry and Gift-Giving Across Borders

This connects directly to a limitation already flagged in our [grandparents' gifting guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-grandparents-gift-guide-two-grandkids) and [registry guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-baby-registry-two-kids-close-in-age): registry-purchased gift bundles (commonly through Babylist) can't ship to Alaska, Hawaii, Canada, or APO/FPO/DPO addresses. For a genuinely international family — say, grandparents in the UK wanting to gift a US-based grandchild, or the reverse — this means the registry-gifting mechanics described in those guides may not work cleanly across your specific border, even when both the buyer and recipient are each within a region Lovevery officially serves.

**The more reliable path for cross-border gifting**, based on the storefront structure above: the gift-giver should generally purchase through whichever regional storefront serves the *recipient's* location, not their own — a UK grandparent gifting a US-based grandchild would use lovevery.com, accepting that they're ordering from a storefront that isn't "theirs," rather than assuming their own region's site can ship the gift across the border. This isn't confirmed as an official recommendation from the company, but it follows logically from the regional-shipping structure documented above, and it avoids the cross-border shipping exclusions that affect registry-specific purchases.

## Currency and Pricing

We don't have confirmed, current pricing for the EU, UK, or Australian storefronts specific enough to state alongside the US tiered pricing ($80/box for the 0-12 month tier, $120/box for 1yr+) used throughout the rest of this content series — pricing on regional storefronts is set in local currency and isn't necessarily a simple currency-converted equivalent of the US figures. If you're budgeting from outside the US, check your regional storefront's current pricing directly rather than converting the US dollar figures used elsewhere in this cluster, since exchange rates, regional pricing strategy, and potential import/customs considerations (relevant specifically if you're using a freight forwarder into a region without an official storefront) can all cause real divergence from a simple currency conversion.

## A Practical Checklist for International Buyers

1. **Identify your correct regional storefront** from the table above before ordering, rather than defaulting to lovevery.com.
2. **If you're outside all four regions**, weigh the freight-forwarding risk described above against your specific desire for this product versus a more locally-available alternative.
3. **Recalculate your expected net cost using informal-channel resale rates**, not the Pre-Loved-based figures used elsewhere in this cluster, since that marketplace won't be available to you.
4. **Verify your regional site's current box lineup** against our box-by-box audit rather than assuming full parity, particularly if specific item names or stage timing matter to your planning.
5. **For cross-border gifting**, purchase through the recipient's regional storefront, not the gift-giver's own region.
6. **Check current regional pricing directly** rather than converting this cluster's US dollar figures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Lovevery.com.au's shipping origin the same as the US site's?**
We couldn't confirm this specifically in this research — the EU and UK sites are confirmed to ship from Germany and the UK respectively, and the US site ships from within the US, but the Australian storefront's shipping origin wasn't documented in the sources we reviewed. Check directly with that storefront if this matters to your planning.

**Does the box-by-box durability audit in this content series apply the same way to items purchased from a regional storefront?**
The underlying materials and construction should be consistent with what's described throughout this content series, since these are the same core developmental design principles across the brand's regional storefronts — though as noted above, we'd recommend confirming your specific regional catalog matches what's described before relying on box-specific detail for planning purposes.

**Can I use Lovevery's Pre-Loved marketplace to buy secondhand internationally, even if I can't sell there?**
No — per the confirmed contiguous-US-only scope, this applies to both buying and selling through that specific platform; it's not accessible from outside that coverage area in either direction.

**If I move from the US to a country served by a different regional storefront, does my existing subscription transfer?**
We don't have documentation confirming cross-storefront account or subscription transfers — given that the four storefronts appear to operate as largely separate regional businesses (different shipping origins, at minimum), it's reasonable to expect you'd need to set up a new subscription through your new region's storefront rather than assuming automatic transfer, though confirming directly with support before an international move is the safer approach.

**Does the twins guide's cost modeling change for international households?**
The core financial models in our [twins guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-for-twins-two-full-subscriptions-or-one-kit) are built around US dollar pricing and the US-specific Pre-Loved resale figures — international households should substitute their regional pricing and the lower informal-resale rates described in this guide when applying those models, rather than using the US figures directly.

**Is there a reason to use a freight forwarder even if my country does have an official storefront, for cost reasons?**
Given the buyer-protection risks described above, we wouldn't recommend a freight forwarder as a cost-optimization strategy if an official storefront already serves your region — the risk (no recourse for lost or damaged shipments) is a real tradeoff that doesn't make sense to accept unless it's the only way to access the product at all.

**Does the storage and rotation framework in this content series need adjustment for international households?**
No — the storage guidance is based on general household organization principles rather than anything region-specific, so it applies the same way regardless of which storefront you're ordering from.

**How do I know if a specific competitor brand (Monti Kids, Hoppi Box, KiwiCo) ships to my region, if Lovevery doesn't?**
We don't have confirmed international shipping documentation for any of the three competitor brands covered in our [competitor comparison guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/beyond-lovevery-monti-kids-hoppi-box-kiwico-panda-crates) — if international availability matters to your decision between brands, checking each one's own shipping policy directly is necessary, since we can't assume parity with Lovevery's regional structure for brands we haven't specifically researched on this dimension.
