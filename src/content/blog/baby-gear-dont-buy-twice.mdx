When two kids are close enough in age that their needs overlap, it's tempting to assume you need two of everything, just offset by a few months. In practice, a fair amount of baby gear can be shared, staggered, or skipped entirely the second time around — you just need to know which items actually require duplicates and which don't.

Preparing for twins or welcoming another baby into the family is an exciting milestone, but the sheer volume of baby gear can quickly overwhelm both your living space and your budget. Marketing often pushes parents into believing two babies require double of absolutely everything — yet practical experience shows that buying duplicates reflexively tends to create clutter and wasted money. The question that clears most of it up: will both kids need this at the literal same moment, regularly? If the honest answer is no, staggering or sharing usually works fine.

Bouncers, swings, and play mats are rarely needed by both kids at the exact same moment — your toddler has usually outgrown the bouncer stage by the time the newborn needs it, which means hand-me-down timing often works in your favor here rather than against it. Instead of buying duplicate swings, buy one swing and one distinct bouncer, rocker, or play mat, then rotate your infants between stations to keep them engaged.
:::

:::tip
**Pro Tip: Activity Rotation** Buy just one swing or bouncer and pair it with a different station — one baby might love a soothing swing motion while the other prefers a stationary bouncy seat or play gym. Because babies get bored easily, having varied activity stations is far more effective than doubling up on the same bulky piece of equipment, and it keeps your living room from disappearing under gear.
:::

## 3. Play Mats & Feeding Accessories

When babies are newborn, a single large play gym or activity mat comfortably accommodates both infants side-by-side. As they grow and begin crawling, play mats transition into general floor space where a single mat remains sufficient.

:::warning
**Worth Knowing: Tandem Feeding Pillows** If you plan to tandem breastfeed or bottle feed at the same time, don't buy two standard single-nursing pillows. Opt for a single specialized twin nursing pillow designed to support two infants at once — and for the wider feeding-timing question across a close age gap, see how to [handle feeding two kids on different schedules](/blog/feeding-schedule-two-different-ages).
:::

Minimalism is ideal for the gear above, but safety standards dictate that certain items must never be shared or swapped unsafely:
- **Car seats:** Every child needs their own properly fitted car seat installed to safety regulations. There's no getting around this one — see [our car seat guide](/blog/car-seat-two-different-sizes) for fitting two different stages in the same car.
- **Cribs / safe sleep surfaces:** If both kids are sleeping at the same time, a bassinet or crib can't be in two places at once. Safe sleep guidelines call for independent, firm surfaces, so you may need both a newborn and a toddler option available at the same time — even a secondhand one or a pack-and-play standing in temporarily.
- **High chairs / booster seats:** Once both kids are eating solids around the table together, both need dedicated secure seating. This sneaks up on people — see our [high chair roundup](/blog/high-chair-roundup), where there's a model that seats two at once. Baby carriers are the one exception where one quality carrier, used by whoever's on duty, usually covers both kids — see our [carrier roundup](/blog/baby-carriers-2026).
:::

- **Buy 1 Only:** Baby swing, infant bathtub, high-capacity diaper bag, play mat.
- **Mix & Match:** Pair 1 swing with 1 bouncer/rocker for station rotation.
- **Specialized Single Purchase:** 1 twin-specific nursing pillow for tandem feeding.
- **Buy 2 Always:** Car seats, individual cribs/bassinets, high chairs.
- **Audit Before Registry:** Check hand-me-downs before finalizing double purchases.
