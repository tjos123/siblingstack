Trying to move a toddler out of the crib because a new baby needs it is the one piece of advice nearly every sleep resource agrees on not doing. But there's a second reality every parent of two kids close in age runs into: sometimes you only have one crib, and the baby is coming whether the toddler is "ready" or not.

Both things are true at once. Here's how to actually manage the timing — and the readiness and safety questions underneath it — so the move doesn't land as a demotion, even when the calendar is forcing your hand.

badge:🧍 Developmental Readiness
badge:📏 Height Benchmarks
badge:🩺 Pediatric Guidance
"Ready" isn't just a feeling — pediatric guidance points to a few concrete signals, and it's worth checking these against your toddler before deciding the timeline is forced:
- **They've climbed out of the crib**, or you can see them attempting it (one leg over the rail counts).
- **The crib mattress is already at its lowest setting** and the top rail sits at less than three-quarters of their height.
- **They've reached roughly 35 inches tall**, the general benchmark pediatric guidance uses for this transition.
- **They're potty training** and need to be able to get up and reach a bathroom on their own overnight.
If none of these are true yet and your toddler is under about 18 months, that's a real signal they're developmentally early for a bed regardless of what the baby's timeline demands — which points you toward the second-crib option below rather than forcing the move. If one or more of these signs is already present, the "too early" concern mostly falls away, and the real question becomes timing relative to the baby, covered next.

:::section
section-message|The real risk isn't the bed, it's the message
badge:🧠 Attachment & Framing
badge:🚼 Sibling Dynamics
A toddler moving to a big-kid bed isn't inherently destabilizing. What's destabilizing is a toddler connecting the dots — consciously or not — that they lost their crib *the same week* the baby arrived and got it. If the timing is forced, the fix isn't waiting for a perfect window that may not exist. It's breaking the visible link between the two events. This is the same reframing that helps with [sibling jealousy when the age gap is small](/blog/sibling-jealousy-close-age-gap): the move needs to feel like something happening *for* the toddler, not *because of* the baby.
:::

:::section
section-timing|Move the bed weeks before the baby, not days before
badge:📅 Timing Strategy
badge:👶 Due-Date Planning
If you know your due date, the single highest-leverage move is transitioning the toddler to a bed at least 4–6 weeks before the baby arrives — enough time that the bed becomes normal and boring before there's a newborn in the house at all. By the time the baby shows up, "I sleep in a big bed" is old news, not a fresh loss layered on top of a new sibling.

If timing doesn't allow that gap, the second-best option is waiting until *after* the baby has been home for a few weeks, so the transition doesn't happen in the same disruptive window as a new sibling, a possible hospital stay, and a house full of visitors.
:::

:::warning
**The same-week collision is the version most likely to cause pushback.** A rushed transition in the same window as the baby's arrival layers a fresh loss on top of a big life change, and it's the timing combination most likely to produce an extended adjustment period.
:::

If your toddler isn't developmentally ready for a bed and the baby's arrival date doesn't leave room for a slow transition, the most reliable fix is not forcing the toddler out early — it's sourcing a second sleep space for the newborn (a bassinet, a secondhand crib, a borrowed one) so the toddler's crib timeline isn't dictated by the baby's arrival at all. This is often cheaper and easier than the alternative: an under-slept toddler and a bed transition that didn't have time to stick. :::section
section-safety|Set the new bed up safely, since the rules change once they can get out on their own
badge:🛡️ Safe Sleep
badge:⚠️ New Rules in Play
A crib is a closed box; a bed isn't, and the safety considerations shift the moment your toddler can climb in and out on their own, day or night.
- **Guardrails.** Fit a rail flush against the mattress and frame with no gap a toddler could slip through, or start with the bed frame low to the ground (or a mattress directly on the floor) if you'd rather skip rails altogether.
- **Firm, fitted mattress.** Same logic as crib safety — avoid soft bedding, loose blankets, and stuffed animals in large numbers, and hold off on a pillow until closer to age 2.
- **Placement matters more now than it did with the crib.** Keep the bed away from windows, blind or curtain cords, heaters, and furniture with sharp corners, since a toddler who can now roam the room at 2am will eventually find whatever's within reach.
- **Secure freestanding furniture to the wall.** A toddler who's mobile in their room overnight, not just in a crib, is also a toddler who can now pull on a dresser or bookshelf. Anti-tip anchors are inexpensive and worth doing at the same time as the bed switch, not as an afterthought.
- **A dim night light and a simple gate at the bedroom door** (if your toddler's prone to wandering) rounds out the setup — the goal isn't to trap them in the room, it's to keep a half-asleep toddler from a stairway or an unlit hallway.
:::

:::section
section-ownership|Let the toddler have ownership over the new bed
badge:🧸 Buy-In
badge:🗳️ Choice & Control
However tight the timing, giving the toddler some choice — which sheets, where the bed goes, picking out one new stuffed animal "for the big bed" — reframes the move as something happening *for* them, not *to* them. This matters more than the exact week it happens. A forced-but-celebrated transition tends to go better than a well-timed but purely functional one.
:::

:::section

Even with ideal timing, some regression around a bed transition is common — trouble staying in bed, more night wake-ups, requests to go back to the crib. This is true whether or not a sibling is involved, so don't assume every wobble is about the baby specifically. Hold the boundary calmly and consistently rather than reversing the transition at the first hard night, which tends to prolong the adjustment rather than shorten it. :::checklist
📋 What to Actually Let Go Of
- **A "perfectly timed" transition doesn't exist** for every family. A workable one that avoids the same-week collision is the realistic goal.
- **Night wake-ups will likely increase for a couple of weeks** after the move. That's normal adjustment, not a sign the toddler wasn't ready.
- **If you do end up moving the toddler out right as the baby arrives** because there was no other option, it's not a parenting failure — it's a logistics constraint, and toddlers are more resilient to a single hard week than the framing around it suggests.
:::

## Common questions

**What's the actual age range for this transition?**
Most children move between 18 months and 3 years, with many families making the switch around age 2 — but readiness signs (climbing out, height, potty training) matter more than the calendar age itself.

**Do I need guardrails on a toddler bed?**
Yes, unless you're using a bed that's already low to the ground or a mattress directly on the floor — a properly fitted guardrail with no gap against the mattress and frame is the standard safety recommendation once a toddler is sleeping in an open bed.

**My toddler isn't showing any readiness signs and the baby's due soon — what do I do?**
Source a second sleep space for the newborn (a bassinet or a second crib) rather than forcing an early bed transition. A toddler who isn't climbing out or asking for a bed yet is generally better served waiting, even if it means buying or borrowing separate gear for the baby in the meantime.
