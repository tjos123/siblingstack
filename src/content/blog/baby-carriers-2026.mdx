## The Tandem-Wearing Question

If you ever need to carry both kids at once — a real scenario in the early months when neither is independently mobile or both need comfort simultaneously — the realistic setup is a front carrier for the newborn (something like the Embrace or Omni 360, positioned high on the chest) paired with a back-carry option for the toddler once they're old enough for back-carrying, generally once they have solid head and neck control and can sit unassisted.

This isn't a single-product solution — it's two carriers used together (the same "both kids need you at once" math behind the [one-on-one time](/blog/one-on-one-time-each-kid-close-in-age) question) — and it's worth [practicing the combination at home](/tools/nap-sync-calculator), briefly and with a second adult nearby the first few times, before relying on it out and about.

:::checklist
📋 Buyer's Action Checklist
-**Skip separate infant inserts:** Choose options with adjustable body panels or soft-knit newborn designs to eliminate extra gear.
-**Check toddler weight limits:** If you want one carrier to last through preschool, ensure a capacity ceiling of at least 45 lbs (e.g., Omni 360 or Tula).
-**Factor in back-carrying:** Ensure your carrier allows back-carrying if you plan on tandem wearing with a toddler.
-**Budget for two stages:** If buying a newborn-only carrier (like the Embrace), plan for a long-haul carrier purchase later.
:::
