**Bottom line up front:** If you're diapering two children at once, the two biggest subscription brands — Hello Bello and The Honest Company — both let you order **two different diaper sizes inside a single monthly bundle**, so you don't have to run two separate subscriptions, pay two separate shipping charges, or manage two separate delivery calendars. What neither brand currently offers, as far as we can confirm from their published subscription mechanics, is granular control over the _exact ratio_ of packs between those two sizes — you pick two sizes, but the split across your pack allotment appears to follow the platform's own allocation rather than a fully custom "3 packs of this, 4 packs of that" slider. That distinction matters enormously for a two-under-two household, and it's the first thing this guide sets straight before walking through the account-level mechanics.

---

## Why This Matters More Than It Sounds Like It Should

Anyone diapering a single child eventually finds a size, locks in a bundle, and forgets about it until the next size-up. Diapering two children at once — especially with an age gap under two years — breaks that rhythm completely. You're now managing two independent consumption curves inside one household budget, and if your subscription can't flex to match both curves, you end up doing exactly what these bundles exist to prevent: emergency Target runs, half-used packs of an outgrown size cluttering the closet, and a monthly bill that no longer reflects what you're actually using.

The two-child dashboard question comes down to three sub-problems:

- **Can one subscription account legally and mechanically hold two different sizes at once?** (Yes, for both major bundle brands, with caveats below.)
- **Can you control how many packs of each size you receive, or is the split fixed?** (This is the part brands are vague about publicly, and where we're being deliberately conservative rather than inventing a workflow we can't verify.)
- **Does mixing sizes cost you any of the bundle discount, design selection, or add-on perks you'd get from a single-size order?** (Generally no — the perks are bundle-level, not size-level.)

## The Step-by-Step Account Portal Walkthrough

A caveat before the walkthrough: subscription-commerce dashboards get redesigned frequently — button labels, menu order, and even the number of sizes you're allowed to mix have changed over the life of both these programs. What's below reflects the subscription structure both companies have publicly described and that subscriber reviews have consistently confirmed over multiple years. Treat the _sequence_ as reliable and the _exact click path_ as something to confirm live in your own account, since a redesign can move things around without notice.

## Hello Bello: Building a Two-Child Bundle

- **Choose your bundle type.** From the Hello Bello bundle builder, you first select the category: Diaper Bundle, Training Pants Bundle, or a Combo Bundle that mixes both. For a household with one child still fully diapered and an older sibling in pull-ups, the Combo path is the one that actually solves your problem — a straight Diaper Bundle keeps you in diapers-only territory.
- **Select your primary size.** Every bundle starts with a base size selection, running from Size N (up to 10 lbs) through Size 6 (35+ lbs).
- **Add the second size.** Hello Bello's own subscription documentation states that parents "can select up to 2 different sizes if they're ordering for more than one child." This is the mechanism that makes a two-under-two household viable on one line item — you are not required to open a second account or a second bundle just because you have two kids in diapers.
- **Choose designs.** You can select up to 7 different prints across the bundle. This step is shared across both sizes — you're not choosing designs separately per child, which is a minor but real limitation if your toddler has strong opinions about dinosaurs versus your infant's unicorn prints.
- **Add extras.** Before checkout, you can attach up to 5 add-on products (wipes refills, rash cream, bath products, vitamins, etc.) at a 15% subscriber discount.
- **First-bundle freebie.** New subscribers get a full-size freebie item added automatically at this stage.
- **Confirm and manage ongoing.** Once the bundle is live, the account dashboard lets you adjust products, shipping date, and frequency at any time — this is also where you'd revisit the size mix as your infant grows out of their current size faster than your toddler does.

**What we can't confirm:** Whether, once you've selected two sizes, you can specify "5 packs of Size 2, 2 packs of Size 4" versus the reverse. Every source describing this feature — including Hello Bello's own bundle copy — talks about selecting _sizes_, not _ratios_. If your two children are at very different volume needs (a two-week-old and a 20-month-old, for example), don't assume the split will match your real-world consumption without checking the live order summary before you confirm checkout. If the split isn't adjustable, your workaround is the one described in the next section.

## The Honest Company: Building a Two-Child Bundle

- **Start your Diapers & Wipes Bundle.** The base structure is 6 packs of diapers plus 4 packs of wipes for one flat monthly price.
- **Select pattern and size per pack.** Subscriber accounts and multiple independent reviews describe the same mechanic consistently over several years: "you can even change the size on just some of the diapers" within your 6-pack allotment, and you can also swap in Overnight or Pull-Up style diapers for any individual pack rather than standard daytime diapers.
- **This is the more granular of the two systems.** Because Honest's bundle is built pack-by-pack rather than as a single size-wide selection, it's the brand more likely to let you approximate a real custom ratio — for example, allocating 4 of your 6 packs to your newborn's size and 2 packs to your toddler's size, simply by setting the size field differently on each pack slot. This has been true of the platform's structure for years, though we'd still recommend confirming the current pack-editing screen shows a size selector per individual pack before you rely on it as your primary two-child solution.
- **Add extras.** Honest allows up to 3–4 additional items added to the bundle at a 25% subscriber discount — a meaningfully deeper discount than Hello Bello's 15%, which matters when you're adding rash cream and a second wipes pack for two kids instead of one.
- **Manage going forward.** Shipping dates and bundle contents can be adjusted from account settings at any time; historically, cancellation required a phone call, though the company has since added online and chat-based cancellation as an alternative.

**Practical implication:** If pack-level granularity is genuinely important to your household — say, a 10-month age gap where one child needs 5 packs' worth of Size 1 and the other only needs 1 pack of Size 4 — Honest's per-pack size selector is structurally better suited to an uneven split than Hello Bello's two-size-selector model, _provided_ the per-pack editing screen still functions the way it has been described. Screenshot your order summary before submitting either bundle so you have a record of what was actually configured, not just what you intended to configure.

## Designs, Wipe Styles, and Where Personalization Breaks Down Across Two Kids

Bundle personalization is heavily marketed by both brands, and it's worth understanding exactly where it applies at the bundle level versus where it would need to apply per-child to be genuinely useful for a two-under-two household — because right now, it's mostly the former.

**Hello Bello** lets you select up to 7 diaper designs per bundle from a rotating catalog that has included more than 25 prints, with new designs introduced most months. That selection is made once, for the bundle as a whole — you're not assigning specific prints to your infant versus your toddler, you're picking a pool of designs that then gets distributed across whichever sizes are in your order. If your toddler has developed a preference (dinosaurs over florals, for instance), you can lean the design pool that direction, but you can't cleanly guarantee "only these three prints go on the Size 4 packs."

**Honest Company** takes personalization in a different direction: rather than a large design catalog, subscribers get to choose from up to 4 wipe formulations — 5 classic wipe styles and 2 nourishing/hydrating options, depending on current catalog — which is more clinically useful for a two-child household than diaper prints. A newborn with sensitive skin and a toddler who doesn't need the gentler formulation can, in principle, be covered by different wipe styles pulled from the same 4-pack wipe allotment, which is a meaningfully practical form of per-child customization that Hello Bello's design-only model doesn't offer.

Neither brand currently advertises the ability to assign a specific design or wipe style to a specific size slot within the same order — if that level of control exists, it isn't part of either company's published subscription description, and we're not going to claim it does.

## Materials, Formulation, and What's Actually in the Pack (for E-E-A-T and Trust)

Since this guide is meant to function as a working reference rather than marketing copy, it's worth being explicit about what's actually documented in each brand's ingredient disclosure, because a two-child household is buying roughly twice the exposure surface of a single-child household and deserves the same level of scrutiny applied twice over.

**Hello Bello diapers** are described as using totally chlorine-free (TCF) fluff pulp sourced from sustainably managed forests, a high-performance super absorbent polymer, polypropylene/polyethylene/polyester nonwoven fibers, a plant-derived PLA nonwoven fiber, spandex strands, and an elastomeric film — and are formulated without chlorine processing, artificial fragrance, phthalates, parabens, lotions, latex, or organotin compounds (TBT/DBT/MBT). The brand's fragrance-free line carries National Eczema Association approval, which is a relevant data point if either of your two children has documented sensitive-skin history.

**Honest Company diapers** are marketed as plant-based and chlorine-free, formulated without common allergens and petrochemicals, and include design features like a wetness indicator, double poo-pockets, and stretchy sides intended to reduce blowout risk — a materials consideration that matters more with two children in diapers simultaneously, since a blowout during a stretch when you're also managing a second child is a meaningfully worse logistics problem than a blowout when you have both hands free.

Neither company publishes a full independent lab-verified ingredient safety report of the kind some newer, more expensive entrants in this category (Coterie, for instance) make part of their marketing. That's not a knock on either brand's formulation — both have been on the market for years with a large subscriber base and no major recurring safety controversy tied to the diapers specifically — but if third-party lab verification is a hard requirement for your household, it's a gap worth knowing about rather than assuming it's covered by the "plant-based" and "chlorine-free" language both brands use.

## Shipping, Delivery Timing, and Coordinating Two Kids' Delivery Windows

A detail that matters more than it initially seems: **shipping cadence is set at the bundle level, not per child.** If you're running one combined two-size bundle, both your infant's and your toddler's diapers arrive on the same schedule, which is convenient for a single delivery event but can be a problem if one child's size is depleting faster than the other's, since you can't independently accelerate delivery for just the higher-consumption child within the same bundle.

Hello Bello's stated shipping structure processes orders within 1–2 business days, with delivery typically arriving 3–7 business days after processing (longer for Alaska, Hawaii, and APO/FPO addresses). Order and delivery-date adjustments are handled from the account's subscription management area, where you can also change frequency — this is the lever to pull if the Volume Calibration Matrix above suggests your household needs a shorter cycle (say, every 21 days instead of every 30) to avoid running short on the higher-use child's size.

Honest Company's subscription similarly allows adjusting shipping dates and bundle contents from account settings at any time. Historically, some subscribers reported that cancellation required a phone call rather than a self-service option, though the company has since added online and chat-based cancellation alternatives — worth confirming directly if a household plans to pause the subscription during, say, a period when daycare is supplying diapers and the household doesn't need a full monthly bundle.

**The two-child workaround for uneven consumption:** since neither brand lets you set independent delivery frequencies for two sizes within one bundle, households with a significant consumption mismatch between their two children (the under-12-month age gaps discussed in the Calibration Matrix) have two realistic options — shorten the entire bundle's delivery frequency to match whichever child is consuming faster (accepting some surplus on the slower-consuming child's size), or split into two separate subscription lines with independently staggered delivery dates, which sacrifices the combined-bundle discount but restores full control over each child's individual cadence.

## The Volume Calibration Matrix

The real test of a two-child bundle isn't whether the portal lets you select two sizes — it's whether the resulting box actually lasts a full month for _both_ kids without stranding you short on one size while sitting on a surplus of the other. Below are three worked scenarios built from each brand's published pack counts and widely corroborated pediatric diaper-frequency ranges (newborns average 8–12 changes/day, infants 4–6 months average 6–9/day, infants 6–12 months average 6–8/day, and toddlers over 12 months average 4–6/day, tapering further as potty training begins).

## Scenario A: 10-Month Age Gap (Irish Twins)

A newborn and a young toddler roughly 10 months apart — for example, a newborn in Size N/1 and a sibling around 10–12 months old in Size 3.

:::table
 Child | Diaper Size | Est. Daily Use | Est. Monthly Need (30 days) 
 Infant | N or 1 | 8–10/day | 240–300 
 Toddler | 3 | 5–6/day | 150–180 
 **Combined household need** | — | — | **390–480/month** 
:::

**Hello Bello check:** A Size N or Size 1 selection alone delivers 238 diapers per bundle — already short of the infant's need on its own before you add a second child. A two-size bundle splitting inventory across N/1 and Size 3 will very likely undershoot the combined 390–480 target for a 10-month gap household unless the split skews heavily toward the higher-volume infant size. **Practical fix:** for this specific gap, plan on a supplemental a-la-carte add-on order or a slightly shortened delivery cycle (every 3 weeks instead of every 4) rather than assuming one standard bundle covers both kids for a full 30 days.

**Honest Company check:** 6 packs total, split by size, at roughly 40–44 diapers per Newborn/Size 1 pack and 34 per Size 3 pack. A 4-pack/2-pack split (4 infant packs, 2 toddler packs) yields roughly 160–176 infant diapers and 68 toddler diapers — again short of the combined need for a full month. The per-pack granularity helps you _aim_ the shortfall toward the size where you have more flexibility (e.g., stretching change intervals slightly for the toddler) but it does not eliminate the underlying math: two kids at 10 months apart consume more than one standard 6-pack bundle provides.

## Scenario B: 15-Month Age Gap

An infant around 6 months old (Size 2) and a toddler around 21 months old (Size 4).

:::table
 Child | Diaper Size | Est. Daily Use | Est. Monthly Need 
 Infant | 2 | 6–8/day | 180–240 
 Toddler | 4 | 4–6/day | 120–180 
 **Combined household need** | — | — | **300–420/month** 
:::

**Hello Bello check:** Size 2 alone provides 217 diapers per bundle; Size 4 alone provides 154. A two-size split drawing from both pools totals 217 + 154 = 371 diapers maximum if you could somehow get a "full" allocation of each — which you can't, since the 7-pack total is shared, not additive, across the two sizes you pick. Realistically, a two-size Hello Bello bundle at this gap will land in the low-to-mid 300s, which sits at the lower edge of the 300–420 combined range. This gap is the closest either brand comes to a genuinely comfortable one-bundle fit, though households on the higher end of typical usage will still want a small buffer stock on hand.

**Honest Company check:** At roughly 40 diapers/pack (Size 2) and 29 diapers/pack (Size 4), a 3-pack/3-pack split delivers about 120 Size 2 diapers and 87 Size 4 diapers — 207 total, noticeably under the 300–420 target. A 4/2 split favoring the higher-use infant size (160 + 58 = 218) doesn't close the gap either. At this gap, both platforms' standard single-bundle volume is more suited to a household using cloth diapers part-time, using daycare-provided diapers for part of the week, or willing to supplement with an a-la-carte top-up.

## Scenario C: 20-Month Age Gap

An older toddler around 26 months old, largely potty-training with occasional diaper/pull-up use (Size 5 or training pants), and a younger sibling around 6 months old (Size 2).

:::table
 Child | Size | Est. Daily Use | Est. Monthly Need 
 Infant | 2 | 6–8/day | 180–240 
 Toddler (potty-training) | 5 / training pants | 2–4/day | 60–120 
 **Combined household need** | — | — | **240–360/month** 
:::

**Hello Bello check:** This is the scenario where a Combo Bundle (diapers + training pants in one order, as described in the portal walkthrough above) earns its keep. Size 2 alone provides 217 diapers; add a modest training-pants allotment for the toddler's remaining accidents and naptime use, and a single bundle comes reasonably close to covering the full household need — closer than either of the two younger-gap scenarios above, because the older child's consumption has dropped substantially.

**Honest Company check:** With the toddler's needs down to occasional coverage, a lopsided pack split (5 packs Size 2, 1 pack of Overnight/Pull-Up swapped in for the toddler) becomes genuinely workable: roughly 200 infant diapers plus one pack's worth of toddler coverage lands inside or very near the 240–360 target range, especially once you factor in that potty-training toddlers often need overnight-only coverage rather than all-day diapering.

**Takeaway across all three scenarios:** the wider the age gap, the more likely a single bundle actually covers both kids for a full billing cycle, simply because the older child's consumption drops faster than the pack-count math can keep pace with a close-together sibling pair. For gaps under about 14–15 months, budget for either a shortened delivery cycle or a standing a-la-carte supplement — don't assume the two-size selector alone solves the volume problem, because in most close-gap households it won't.

## The "Add-On" Financial Ledger

The subscriber discount on add-ons is where a two-child household actually recovers some of the shortfall identified above, because both platforms apply the discount to items well beyond diapers themselves.

**Hello Bello's math:** The standard bundle (7 packs diapers + 4 packs wipes) is priced at $79.99, against a combined non-bundle retail value the brand itself lists at $85.85 — a modest built-in saving of roughly $6 before you add anything else. On top of that, subscribers get 15% off up to 5 additional items per bundle, drawn from personal care, home care, outdoor, apparel, and vitamin categories. For a two-child household, the realistic play is routing recurring essentials — a second pack of wipes, rash cream, baby wash — through this add-on slot instead of a separate store run, since you're getting a guaranteed 15% off list price on items you were already going to buy monthly regardless of which child they're for.

**Honest Company's math:** Honest's add-on discount is deeper — 25% off up to 3–4 additional items per bundle — which meaningfully changes the calculation for households buying rash cream or a second wipes pack every month. A 25% recurring discount on a $10–15 rash cream, applied monthly across a year, adds up to real money saved versus buying the same product retail or through a non-subscription online order. The trade-off is that Honest's base bundle price has moved around more over the years than Hello Bello's — subscriber reviews and product pages place it anywhere from the high $70s to just under $90 depending on when you check — so the _percentage_ discount on add-ons is the more stable number to plan around, not the sticker price of the base bundle itself, which you should verify at checkout every time rather than assume from a past order.

**The consolidation case for two kids specifically:** the strongest financial argument for using bundle add-ons instead of separate shopping trips isn't the discount percentage in isolation — it's the elimination of decision fatigue and emergency purchases. A household managing two diapering children is already running a tighter logistics margin than a single-child household; every product category you can fold into one guaranteed monthly delivery (wipes, rash cream, baby wash) is one fewer thing that can run out unexpectedly at 11 p.m. with two kids awake. Measured only in dollars, the 15–25% add-on discounts are worthwhile but not dramatic. Measured in avoided pharmacy runs during a week when you're already stretched between two children's needs, the value is considerably higher — it's just not a number either brand publishes, so treat it as a qualitative factor in your decision rather than something you can benchmark against a specific dollar figure.

## Building Your Own Household-Specific Calibration

The three scenarios above use round-number age gaps to illustrate the method, but your household's actual numbers will differ, and the method matters more than the specific figures. Here's the calculation to run yourself before configuring either bundle:

- **Track actual daily changes for 3–5 days per child**, rather than relying on published averages. The ranges cited throughout this guide (8–12/day for newborns, tapering to 4–6/day by 12 months, and further down through potty training) are wide for a reason — actual usage varies by feeding pattern, climate, and individual bladder capacity. A household logging 11 changes/day for a 2-month-old needs meaningfully more volume than one logging 8.
- **Multiply by 30 (or your actual billing cycle length)** to get each child's monthly need, then add the two figures together, exactly as done in the tables above.
- **Compare against the specific brand's published per-size pack count** for the sizes you'll actually be ordering — not a generic "diapers per bundle" figure, since count-per-pack drops substantially as size increases for both brands (roughly halving from Newborn to Size 6 in Honest's case, and dropping by half between Size N and Size 6 for Hello Bello as well).
- **Build in a 10–15% buffer** rather than ordering to the exact calculated total. Growth spurts, illness-related diaper changes, and travel days all push consumption above baseline, and a subscription that's calibrated to the bare minimum will run short during exactly the weeks you have the least bandwidth to deal with it.
- **Re-run the calculation every 8–10 weeks**, or sooner if either child crosses a size threshold. Infant growth in the first year is fast enough that a calibration done in month one is often stale by month three.

This is the same underlying math used in the three worked scenarios above — the value of doing it yourself with real logged numbers, rather than relying on published averages alone, is that it accounts for your specific children's actual consumption rather than a population-wide range that may not reflect either of them closely.

## Practical Recommendations by Household Type

- **If your two children are less than 12 months apart:** Neither brand's standard single bundle will fully cover combined volume most months. Plan on either a shortened delivery cycle, a standing a-la-carte top-up order, or splitting into two separate subscription lines timed to arrive on different weeks of the month so you're never waiting on both sizes at once.
- **If your gap is roughly 14–20 months:** You're in the sweet spot where a single two-size bundle gets close to covering real household need, particularly once the older child begins tapering diaper use. Honest's per-pack size control gives you a slight edge here if your two children's consumption is meaningfully uneven; Hello Bello's simpler two-size selector is easier to manage if consumption is closer to even.
- **If your older child is actively potty training:** Move to a Combo Bundle (Hello Bello) or swap a portion of your pack allotment to Pull-Up/training-pant style (Honest) rather than continuing to order standard diapers for a child who's using them inconsistently — this is the single highest-leverage adjustment available in either portal for a household in transition.
- **Regardless of brand:** re-check your bundle configuration at least once a quarter. Growth curves are unpredictable, and a size mix that fit perfectly three months ago is a common source of the exact overstock/understock problem these bundles are supposed to solve.
- **If you're reading this because you're considering cancelling your subscription over a size or volume mismatch:** you likely don't need to — everything above is the fix. Cancellation is the right tool when a household is done with diapers entirely or switching brands, not when the underlying issue is a size mismatch the dashboard is built to handle. If you do need to cancel for a genuine end-of-subscription reason, [the Hello Bello cancellation guide](/blog/hello-bello-cancellation-exit-strategy) walks through how to do that safely, including how to avoid getting billed for a shipment you no longer need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get two different diaper sizes in one Hello Bello bundle?**
Yes. Hello Bello's bundle builder explicitly allows selecting up to two different sizes within a single monthly bundle, intended for households ordering for more than one child.

**Can I control the exact number of packs per size in a two-size bundle?**
This isn't clearly documented by either brand. Hello Bello's public description refers to selecting sizes, not setting a custom pack ratio. Honest Company's per-pack size-and-style selector is structurally more likely to support an uneven split, since you're editing each of the 6 packs individually rather than choosing from a bundle-wide size list — but confirm the live behavior in your own account before relying on it.

**Will mixing sizes cost me any of the subscriber discount?**
No. The bundle price and the add-on discount percentage are tied to the bundle itself, not to ordering a single size, for both brands as currently structured.

**Can I swap a diaper pack for training pants without starting a new subscription?**
Yes for both brands. Hello Bello offers a dedicated Combo Bundle mixing diapers and training pants; Honest allows swapping Overnight or Pull-Up style products in for any individual pack within the existing bundle structure.

**What happens if one of my children outgrows their size mid-cycle?**
Both platforms allow adjusting size, shipping date, and bundle frequency from the account dashboard at any time before the next order processes — there's no need to cancel and restart to change a size selection.

**Is a two-size bundle enough diapers for two children a full month?**
It depends heavily on the age gap. Based on the pack-count math in this guide, gaps under roughly 12–14 months typically fall short of combined household need in a standard single bundle; wider gaps, especially once the older child begins tapering usage, come much closer to full coverage.

**Do the wipes included in the bundle count toward the discount, or are they separate?**
The 4 packs of wipes are included in the base bundle price for both brands — they're not treated as a discounted add-on, they're part of the standard package alongside the diapers.

**Is it cheaper to run two separate single-size subscriptions instead of one mixed bundle?**
Generally no, because you'd pay two separate monthly base charges (and, in some cases, two shipping fees) instead of one. A single two-size bundle is the more cost-efficient structure whenever the brand supports it — the tradeoff is the volume-matching limitation described in the Calibration Matrix above, not the price itself.

**How do I know which size split my bundle actually shipped with?**
Check your order confirmation email and the account dashboard's order history immediately after checkout, before the order enters fulfillment — this is the most reliable way to catch a misconfigured split before it ships, since the live editing screens can change their layout without notice.

**Can I choose different wipe formulations for each child in the same bundle?**
Honest Company's wipe selection (up to 4 styles, including nourishing/hydrating options) makes this more realistic than Hello Bello's, which doesn't currently offer wipe-style customization at all. Neither brand explicitly guarantees you can assign a specific wipe style to a specific child's packs, but Honest's broader style catalog gives you more raw material to work with.

**Does a two-size bundle cost more than a single-size bundle?**
No — based on both brands' published pricing structures, the bundle price is fixed regardless of how many sizes you select within it (up to the stated limit of two for Hello Bello, or across your 6 individually editable packs for Honest). You're not charged extra for mixing sizes.

**Should I keep a small emergency stock outside the subscription?**
For any household with an age gap under roughly 14 months, yes. The Volume Calibration Matrix above shows combined need regularly exceeding a single bundle's pack count at these gaps — a small buffer pack purchased retail, especially in the faster-outgrowing infant size, removes the risk of a mid-cycle stockout while you dial in the right delivery frequency.
