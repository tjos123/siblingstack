# Should You Put Lovevery on Your Baby Registry If You're Planning for Two Kids Close in Age?

**Bottom line up front:** yes, with a specific strategic twist most registry advice won't tell you — if you already know you're planning for two kids close together, the smartest registry move isn't trying to register separately for a child who doesn't exist yet. It's registering generously for your first child's Play Kits, because per the pass-down mechanics covered throughout this content series, a bigger investment in child one's subscription indirectly funds a meaningful head start for child two once they arrive. This article covers both the registry mechanics that make Lovevery work well as a registry item, and the strategic sizing question that's specific to knowing a close-age sibling is already part of the plan.

This is written for the person building the registry — if you're the one buying a gift for someone else's two close-in-age kids, our [grandparents' gifting guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-grandparents-gift-guide-two-grandkids) covers that side of the same product instead.

## Why This Is a Different Question Than "Should I Buy Lovevery"

Every other cost-focused article in this content series assumes you're the one paying. A registry decision is a different kind of question entirely: you're not asking "is this worth my money," you're asking "is this a reasonable thing to ask other people to fund, and will they actually follow through." Those are genuinely different calculations, and it's worth treating them separately rather than assuming a good personal-budget case automatically makes a good registry case.

## The Registry Mechanics That Actually Make This Work

Lovevery's gifting structure, covered in detail in our [grandparents' guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-grandparents-gift-guide-two-grandkids), happens to be well-suited to registry use specifically, for reasons that aren't true of every subscription product:

**It's sold as discrete, one-time bundles, not an open-ended recurring charge to the gift-giver.** A guest purchasing "3 Play Kits" through your registry is making a single, fixed-price purchase — they're not signing up to be billed repeatedly the way a true ongoing subscription gift would require. This matters for registry compatibility specifically, since guests are generally comfortable with a one-time purchase in a way they're not with an open-ended recurring commitment on your behalf.

**It's genuinely listed on major registry platforms**, commonly Babylist, as a real, purchasable registry item — not something you'd have to add as a generic cash-fund request. This gives it the same "add to cart, buy, ship" simplicity as any other registry item, which lowers the friction for guests compared to items that require an external purchase and manual registry update.

**The activation-flexibility feature covered in our grandparents' guide applies here too.** Since whoever receives the gift enters the baby's actual birth date at activation rather than the gift being locked to a stage at purchase time, registry gifts purchased months before your due date — or even purchased and then not activated for a while after birth — still arrive correctly matched to your baby's actual current stage, not whatever stage they'd have been at when a well-meaning guest clicked "buy."

## The Two-Kids-Close-in-Age Strategic Angle

Here's the part that's genuinely different if you already know you're planning for two kids close together, rather than deciding one child at a time.

Registries exist for the child on the way — you can't register for a second child who isn't conceived or born yet. But per the sibling pass-down mechanics covered in our [core cost math guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit), a subscription that runs longer for child one produces more material that eventually benefits child two, provided your actual gap lands in the 12-24 month range this content series identifies as the strongest overlap window. That reframes the registry-sizing question in a useful way: **rather than registering for a modest number of kits and assuming you'll separately handle child two's needs later, registering more generously for child one is functionally a two-child investment**, made through a registry mechanism that only works for one child at a time on paper.

Concretely: per the gift-bundle guidance referenced in our grandparents' guide, two gift bundles purchased together cover a full first year of the 0-12 month tier. If you're planning a close-age second child, registering for that full first year (rather than a smaller, single starter bundle) means your older child moves through more of the early, highest-durability, easiest-to-pass-down boxes — per our [box-by-box audit](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit), boxes like The Looker, The Charmer, and The Senser are among the cleanest pass-down items in the entire lineup — before your second child even arrives. That's real, practical registry-planning value that a generic "just register for whatever you want" approach misses.

## Is It "Too Much" to Register For? The Honest Etiquette Question

This is worth addressing directly rather than assuming every registrant will feel equally comfortable with it. Traditional registry etiquette leans toward essentials first — car seats, a crib, feeding gear — with toys and enrichment items sometimes treated as secondary, lower-priority registry asks. Lovevery's price point ($80-120 per kit, real bundle prices in the $228+ range for multi-kit gifts) puts it closer to a mid-tier gear item than a typical toy registry entry, which is worth being upfront about rather than assuming every guest will treat it the same as a $15 toy request.

A few things work in its favor as a registry item despite that price positioning:

- **It's explicitly developmental and educational in its framing**, not purely recreational — many registrants and gift-givers treat "developmental toys" as falling closer to the essentials category than pure entertainment items, softening some of the traditional toys-are-secondary registry etiquette.
- **The price point suits group gifting well.** Most registry platforms, Babylist included, support multiple guests contributing toward a single higher-ticket item rather than requiring one person to cover the full cost — a natural fit for a $228+ multi-kit bundle, the same way group gifting commonly works for strollers or car seats.
- **You're not obligated to make it a large ask.** A single, smaller kit gift, or an e-gift card in a modest amount, are both legitimate lower-commitment registry entries if you're not comfortable asking for a full-year bundle outright.

If you're genuinely unsure whether this fits your specific shower or gifting circle's norms, a reasonable middle path is registering for a modest number of kits alongside an e-gift card of a smaller, flexible amount — giving guests both a defined, one-time item to purchase and a lower-commitment option, without committing your entire registry's "nice-to-have" allocation to one brand.

## The Timing Risk Registries Specifically Create

Worth flagging honestly: registries introduce a timing gap that direct purchases don't. A guest might buy a registry item weeks before your shower, months before your due date, or — in practice, for guests who wait — sometimes well after the baby has already arrived. Per the mechanics covered in our grandparents' guide, the activation-at-birth-date system handles most of this cleanly, since the correct stage gets selected whenever you actually activate the gift, not when it was purchased.

The one real edge case worth knowing: if your child ages out of a specific tier (the 0-12 month range, for gifts tied to that bracket specifically) before a multi-kit registry gift has been fully used — which can happen if gifts are purchased and activated later than expected, or if your child was born earlier than your due date — Lovevery has a documented process for converting the unused portion into store credit applicable to later-stage kits, rather than the remaining value simply going to waste. Worth knowing this safety net exists, but also worth mentioning in a note to guests (if your registry platform allows gift-giver notes) that earlier purchases generally give the system more runway to be used as originally intended.

## Practical Registry Setup Recommendations

Pulling this into an actual plan:

1. **If you're already planning a close-age second child, register generously for your first child's early-stage kits** — per the strategic case above, this indirectly benefits child two more than trying to reserve registry space for a not-yet-existing second registry.
2. **Consider two multi-kit bundles rather than one**, if your registry budget allows, to cover a full first year at the 0-12 month tier rather than a partial one.
3. **Add a smaller e-gift card as a flexible, lower-commitment option** alongside your main kit registration, for guests who'd rather contribute a partial amount than commit to a full bundle.
4. **Use group gifting if your registry platform supports it**, particularly for larger multi-kit bundles, rather than assuming one guest needs to cover the full cost alone.
5. **Note in your registry (if your platform allows a gift-giver message) that earlier purchases and activations work best** — this isn't required, but it reduces the odds of hitting the store-credit conversion scenario described above.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I register for the Play Gym instead of, or alongside, the Play Kits?**
Both are reasonable registry entries — per our [Play Gym guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-play-gym-two-under-two-one-gym-two-babies), it's a single one-time purchase at a fixed price ($150), which is arguably an even more straightforward registry item than the Play Kits bundles, since there's no activation-timing or stage-matching consideration involved at all.

**What if I don't yet know for certain that I'll have a second child close in age?**
The strategic sizing argument in this article still holds reasonably well even without certainty — a generously-sized Play Kits registration benefits your first child regardless of whether a second child arrives, and if one does arrive within the pass-down-friendly window, you get the added benefit described above; if not, you haven't lost anything by registering generously for your first child specifically.

**Is it better to register through Babylist directly or through Lovevery's own site?**
If your registry platform is already Babylist, registering there keeps everything on one platform for your guests, which reduces friction. If you're using a different registry platform without native Lovevery integration, Lovevery's own direct gifting flow (covered in our grandparents' guide) is a reliable fallback, though it means the item won't show up natively inside your primary registry's guest-facing list.

**Will guests be confused about what they're actually buying if they've never heard of Lovevery?**
This is a reasonable concern for a less mainstream brand relative to typical registry staples — a brief note in your registry description explaining what the Play Kits are and why you've included them can meaningfully reduce guest hesitation, since most registry confusion comes from unfamiliarity rather than the price itself.

**Does registering for Lovevery make sense if I'm planning to breastfeed/have limited baby-proofing space/any other unrelated registry consideration?**
Those considerations don't meaningfully interact with the Lovevery registry decision specifically — this is a developmental-toy registry item, and its value case rests on the stage-matching and sibling-math points covered throughout this article and the rest of this content series, independent of feeding method or home setup.

**If I end up not needing all the registered kits because of generous outright purchases from close family, what happens to the extra registry gifts?**
Per the gift-activation flexibility discussed above, unused gift codes simply remain available to activate whenever they're needed — there's no forced immediate use, so "too many" registered kits isn't really a wasted-gift problem the way an unused physical item might be.

**Is there a risk that registering for two kits' worth of gifts looks presumptuous before my second child is even conceived?**
Framed correctly on your registry (as an investment in your first child's subscription, not an explicit "for future sibling" request), this shouldn't read as presumptuous to guests — you're registering for real, immediate use by the child who's actually arriving, and the sibling benefit described in this article is simply a smart secondary effect of that choice, not something you need to explain or justify to your guests.

**Can multiple guests split the cost of a single multi-kit bundle instead of one person covering it alone?**
Yes, per the group-gifting functionality discussed above — most registry platforms, Babylist included, support several guests contributing toward one higher-priced item rather than requiring a single purchaser, which is worth highlighting in your registry notes if you're registering for a larger bundle and want to make that option visible to guests who might otherwise hesitate at the full price.
