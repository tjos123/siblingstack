A toddler who's barely settled into being the only child is suddenly sharing your attention with a newborn — and unlike a bigger age gap, where the older kid might feel some pride or independence about it, a small age gap often means your toddler is still developmentally very attached to you, with limited language to express what they're feeling. Here's how to navigate envy, protect schedule harmony, and build a bond when two kids are close in age.

:::section
why-jealousy|Why Sibling Jealousy Spikes with Close Age Gaps
badge:👶 Age Gap: Under 24 Months
badge:⚖️ Risk: High Schedule Friction
When siblings are spaced less than two years apart, the older child often hasn't fully outgrown their own "needy baby" stage. They lack the emotional regulation and complex language skills needed to express feelings of envy or displacement effectively.
- **Regression over aggression:** With a small age gap, jealousy more often shows up as regression than outright aggression — wanting a bottle again, asking to be carried, sudden potty-training setbacks, or wanting to be fed the way they see the baby being fed.
- **Clinginess as anxiety:** It can also show up as clinginess that looks like the opposite of jealousy: a toddler who suddenly won't let you out of their sight isn't being naughty, they're often anxious about whether your attention is still reliably theirs.
:::

:::warning
**Worth Knowing: The 18-Month Threshold:** Sibling envy rises significantly when children are under 24 months apart. The older sibling perceives the shift in family dynamics without the full language capacity to negotiate it — which is why the attention battles feel so intense right now.
:::

## What it looks like at this age

Genuine aggression toward the baby does happen too, and it's common enough not to be alarming on its own — but it's worth taking seriously enough to never leave a toddler unsupervised with the newborn during this adjustment period. The regression-driven behaviors above are usually a toddler telling you, in the only language they have, that they need reassurance that your attention is still theirs.

:::section
schedule-alignment|Mitigating Rivalry Through Schedule Alignment
badge:⏰ Schedule Sync
badge:💡 Actionable Strategy
Much of early sibling jealousy isn't purely psychological — it's situational. When a newborn's feeding window directly collides with an older toddler's pre-nap meltdown, stress peaks for everyone.
By visualizing both children's daily patterns side-by-side, parents can identify friction points before they escalate into high-stress moments. Protect a dedicated 15-minute one-on-one block with your older child during the baby's regular morning nap — predictable individual attention dramatically reduces attention-seeking behavior.
:::

:::tip
**Pro Tip: Preserve One-on-One Windows:** A short, reliable solo block with the older child — even five to ten minutes — tends to reduce anxiety-driven behavior more than occasional longer stretches that happen unpredictably. Consistency beats duration.
:::

## Practical interventions for everyday moments

Instead of scolding negative reactions, focus on building emotional awareness and positive associations. Name what you notice, simply, without over-explaining — a short, accurate label is often more effective than a long reassurance.

:::section
everyday-moments|Practical Interventions for Everyday Moments
badge:💬 Communication
badge:❤️ Empathy Building
- **Involve without forcing:** Let your eldest pick the baby's blanket or pass a diaper, rewarding them with specific praise.
- **Validate feelings:** Use simple language like, "It's hard to wait when I'm feeding the baby. You wish we could play right now."
- **Avoid automatic blame:** Say "my hands are busy" instead of "the baby is making us late."
- **Don't force affection:** "Go give the baby a kiss" when a toddler isn't in the mood builds resentment rather than bonding.
- **Expect it to ebb and flow:** A toddler can seem fine for a week and regress again, often around a new developmental stage of their own — our [milestones article](/blog/milestones-10-month-age-gap) walks through how those stages resync with a small age gap.
:::

:::checklist
✅ Sibling Jealousy Action Checklist
-Establish a predictable 15-minute one-on-one period daily with your older child.
-Map nap and feed schedules on a dual timeline to spot overlap chaos.
-Use neutral phrasing when delays happen (avoid framing the baby as the issue).
-Give your toddler practical mini-jobs during daily routines.
-Acknowledge and validate your toddler's feelings when frustration arises.
:::

Most sibling adjustment difficulty at this age is developmentally normal and settles within a few months. If aggression toward the baby is frequent, intense, or not easing over time — or if you're finding it hard to manage your own stress — it's worth talking to your paediatrician. They can help distinguish ordinary adjustment from something that warrants more support.
