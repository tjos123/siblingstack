## 1. The Baseline Math

Having two kids in diapers simultaneously scales differently than single-child advice suggests. A newborn typically goes through roughly 8–12 diapers a day, dropping to around 6–8 by a few months old; a toddler is usually down to 4–6 a day. Combined, you're realistically looking at 12–18 changes a day across both kids during the newborn stage — not double a single kid's count, but close to it in the first couple of months.

Diaper pricing varies widely by brand and whether you buy in bulk, but a reasonable planning range for two kids combined during the newborn-plus-toddler stage is often somewhere in the $100–180/month territory if you're using name-brand disposables, less if you mix in store brands or subscribe-and-save pricing, more if you're brand-loyal to premium options. This is a wide range on purpose — your actual number depends heavily on brand choice, and it's worth tracking your real spending for the first month rather than trusting any single estimate, including this one.

## 2. Where the Real Savings Opportunities Are

**Buying two sizes at once, in bulk, when you know you'll need both.** If you can predict your toddler's size holding steady for the next few months, buying their size in bulk alongside the newborn's smaller size in bulk often unlocks better per-diaper pricing than buying each size separately as you run low. The same buy-ahead logic applies to clothing and shoes — our [hand-me-down sizing cheat sheet](/blog/hand-me-down-sizing-cheat-sheet) breaks down which sizes are actually safe to stock up on.

**Subscription/auto-ship discounts**, which are frequently the single biggest lever — often in the 15–20% range off list price — and matter more here than for a single-kid household because you're ordering enough volume to make the discount meaningful.

**Sizing up early on the newborn, not late.** Diapers that are slightly too big leak less often than diapers that are too small, and a newborn grows out of the smallest sizes within weeks regardless — buying ahead at the next size up isn't usually wasted money the way it might be with clothing.

:::warning
**⚠️ Watch the Wipes:** Wipes — not diapers — are where silent budget waste occurs. Two kids in diapers means significantly more wipe usage than people budget for, since wipes get used for hands, faces, food spills, and general cleanup far beyond diaper changes once there's a toddler in the mix.
:::

## 3. What Doesn't Actually Save Money

**Cloth diapering one kid and disposables for the other**, as a half-measure to cut costs. The laundry time cost (more loads, more often, with two kids' worth of mess already in the house) tends to outweigh the diaper savings unless you're committed to cloth for both kids and have already built the routine around it.

**Buying diapers far in advance for the toddler "just in case."** Toddlers' sizes (and sometimes their readiness for potty training) change faster than expected, and an oversized stockpile of a size they've outgrown is a real, common waste — more so with a second kid in the house competing for storage space and budget.

## The bottom line

Plan for a noticeable jump in diaper spend, not a doubling — the toddler's lower daily count offsets some of the newborn's higher one. Track your actual spend for the first month rather than budgeting blind, since brand and size choices swing the real number more than any general estimate can capture.
