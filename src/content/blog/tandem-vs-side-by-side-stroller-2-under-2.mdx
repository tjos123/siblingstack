Choosing a double stroller is one of the biggest gear decisions you'll make when expecting your second baby. With an infant and a toddler under two, your daily outings depend entirely on your wheels. The eternal debate comes down to two major configurations: **tandem (in-line)** vs. **side-by-side**. But when you're [planning strollers](/blog/double-stroller-close-in-age) around two kids under two, the decision usually comes down to one core question that most buying guides skip: **which layout actually protects naps better once you're out of the house?**

Each style solves different problems — tandem models squeeze through tight doorways while side-by-side models offer equal views and prevent backseat bickering. Below we break down the weight, steering performance, pros, and cons so you can choose the right rig for your family.

Tandem strollers place one child in front of the other (or stadium-style, with one seat elevated above the other). Because they match the width of a single stroller, you rarely have to worry about getting stuck in grocery store checkouts or standard doorways. For naps, the infant/bassinet position is often more enclosed and shaded, and the seats don't share a connected recline mechanism — so the front seat can go fully flat for the baby regardless of what the toddler seat is doing.
- **The Pros:** Fit through narrow single doorways, elevators, and café aisles effortlessly; easier to navigate on crowded public transit or narrow city sidewalks; many convert easily back into single strollers when your older child wants to walk.
- **The Cons:** Steering feels heavier, especially when turning corners or mounting curbs due to weight distribution; the child in the back seat (often the toddler) has an obstructed view; storage baskets can be difficult to access when both seats are fully occupied.
:::

:::warning
**Worth Knowing:** Look for "stadium seating" tandem models where the rear seat is elevated. If the seats are flat inline, your toddler in the back will stare directly at the back of the infant seat with zero visibility — great for nap protection, but a poor fit for a curious, bored toddler.
:::

Side-by-side strollers sit both children next to each other. While they carry a reputation for being wide "battleships," modern side-by-side double strollers (like the Mountain Buggy Nano Duo or Bumbleride Indie Twin) have slimmed down dramatically to fit through standard 32-inch commercial doorways. They also give both kids equal, unobstructed sightlines — which some toddlers find more engaging, reducing boredom noise during the baby's on-the-go naps.
- **The Pros:** Exceptional weight distribution makes them push and steer with one hand, even over rough terrain; both children get an equal view of the world; less fighting over "who gets to sit where" as they grow older.
- **The Cons:** Can struggle with older, narrow historic doorways, turnstiles, and tight retail aisles; takes up a massive footprint in your car trunk when folded; the two close-together seats mean a wiggly awake toddler can bump, kick, or talk directly next to a sleeping baby.
:::

:::warning
**Pro Tip:** Always measure the front door of your house, your garage entrance, and the trunk of your car *before* purchasing a side-by-side model. A stroller that won't fit through your front door is a daily headache — and the same tape-measure check often narrows the tandem-vs-side-by-side decision more than any general rule.
:::

verdict-section|Which One Should You Choose?
badge:💡 Decision Framework
Your ideal configuration depends entirely on your lifestyle geography:
- **Choose a Tandem if:** You live in a dense urban area, frequently use public transport, love shopping in boutique stores with narrow aisles, drive a compact car with limited trunk space — or you rely on stroller naps for your baby during daily outings.
- **Choose a Side-by-Side if:** You live in the suburbs, walk primarily in neighborhoods and parks, value easy steering and maneuverability above all else, have a vehicle with a wide trunk — and your toddler is calm enough not to bother a sleeping sibling next to them.
:::

## A hybrid option worth knowing

Some families solve this by using a [single stroller plus a compatible infant car seat](/blog/car-seat-two-different-sizes) or bassinet attachment for the early months (functionally similar to a tandem's nap protection), then switching to a [side-by-side or standard double](/blog/double-stroller-roundup) once the baby is past the heaviest nap-on-the-go phase. This avoids buying a full tandem system that may only be optimal for a relatively short window.

## Where side-by-side wins beyond steering

- **Sightlines and connection.** Both kids can see each other, you, and the world equally, which some toddlers find more engaging and less likely to cause boredom fuss.
- **Even weight distribution.** The balanced left-right push handles uneven sidewalks and grass more predictably than the front-back weight of a tandem.
- **Simpler seat swapping as kids grow.** Both seats are usually similar in size and shape, making it easier to move an older toddler into either seat as your second child grows.

## Where tandem wins beyond naps

- **Doorway and aisle width.** Tandem strollers are narrower, handling grocery store aisles, elevators, and narrower home doorways with ease.
- **Weather and sun protection.** The enclosed feel of many tandem infant positions blocks more wind, sun, and rain than an open side-by-side seat.
- **One-handed steering.** The narrower footprint of most tandems is noticeably easier to steer one-handed — relevant if you're also holding a toddler's hand, a diaper bag, and a coffee.

## A practical framework for deciding

Ask yourself these questions in order:

1. **Do you rely on stroller naps for your baby regularly (daily walks, errands during nap windows)?** If yes, lean tandem.
2. **Do you have narrow doorways, small elevators, or tight store aisles you navigate often?** If yes, lean tandem.
3. **Is your toddler generally calm and unlikely to bother a sleeping sibling next to them?** If yes, side-by-side becomes more viable.
4. **Do you prioritize both kids being able to see each other and interact during rides?** If yes, lean side-by-side.
5. **How's your trunk space?** Measure your actual trunk with a tape measure against the folded dimensions of specific models — this often narrows the decision more than any general rule.

## FAQ

**Is a tandem stroller harder to push than a side-by-side?**
Not usually — most tandems are narrower and considered easier to steer one-handed, though they can feel longer and slightly harder to turn in very tight spaces like small shops.

**Can a newborn nap well in a side-by-side stroller?**
Yes, especially with a bassinet attachment or a seat that reclines fully flat, but the baby's seat being directly next to an active toddler seat is the main risk factor for disrupted naps in this layout.

**Which is better for uneven sidewalks or trail walking?**
Side-by-side strollers tend to have more even, predictable weight distribution for pushing over uneven terrain, though it varies by wheel size and suspension across specific models.
