When two kids are close in age, "can I just use what I already have" comes up constantly — clothes, shoes, gear, all of it. The honest answer is: sometimes yes, sometimes it costs you more in frustration than it saves in money. Here's a practical breakdown by category.

## Clothing

**Onesies and basics: hand down freely, but buy ahead in the next size up.** Because your kids are close in age, your older child will often still be wearing a size your younger one is about to grow into — meaning hand-me-downs frequently arrive *too late* rather than too early. Buying the next size up before you need it, rather than waiting to hand things down in sequence, usually works better in practice than a strict rotation.

**Seasonal clothing is the actual trap.** If your kids are 10–14 months apart, the size your older child wore last summer may not be the size your younger one needs this summer, even if the numerical size matches — kids grow at different rates, and a hand-me-down that's "the right size" on paper can still be the wrong season's weight of fabric. Check actual measurements, not just size labels, before assuming a hand-me-down works.

**Shoes: don't assume sizing transfers at all.** Foot width and shape vary kid to kid even within the same family, and a hand-me-down shoe that fits in length can still be wrong in width. Always re-measure rather than assuming the size that worked for one kid will work for the other.

## Gear that genuinely transfers

- **Bath tub inserts** — almost always reusable, low wear
- **Swaddles and sleep sacks** — reusable as long as you're checking current weight/mobility guidance for safe use at your younger child's stage, since safe sleep recommendations are based on the baby's development, not just the product's size label
- **Bouncers, play mats, activity gyms** — low wear, easy to reuse, and your older child has usually outgrown the need for these by the time your younger one arrives
- **Most toys** — obvious, but worth saying: there's no reason to buy these twice

## Gear that needs its own copy, even close in age

- **Car seats** — safety equipment with expiration dates and individual fit requirements; see our [convertible car seat guide](/blog/convertible-car-seats-2026) for what to actually look at
- **Crib/bassinet, if both kids are sleeping at the same time** — can't be in two places at once
- **Toothbrushes, hairbrushes, anything hygiene-related** — obvious, but the most commonly forgotten "obviously separate" item when you're running on no sleep

## The "wears out fast" category — buy once, but buy well

Items in heavy daily rotation — bibs, burp cloths, the crib sheet you're washing constantly — degrade faster than people expect, especially with two kids' worth of use. Buying a higher quantity of a mid-tier option tends to work out better here than buying a small number of premium ones, since you'll be replacing them on wear regardless of original price point.

## A simple rule of thumb

Before buying a second of anything, ask: will both kids need this at the literal same moment, regularly, for more than a few weeks? If yes — car seats, simultaneous sleep surfaces, daily-wear clothing in the season they actually overlap — buy two. If no, stagger, hand down, or buy ahead rather than buying double.
