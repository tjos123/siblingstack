# Grandparents' Guide to Buying Lovevery for Two Grandkids Close in Age (Without Overpaying)

**Bottom line up front:** Lovevery's gifting system has one feature that solves the biggest worry most grandparents have before buying: you don't need to know exactly what stage either grandchild is on at the moment you purchase. The recipient enters the baby's actual birth date (or adjusted birth date, for a premature grandchild) when they activate the gift, which automatically matches the correct starting box — meaning you can buy today without calling to ask "what stage is the baby in right now?" and risking an awkward or outdated answer by the time the gift ships. The harder question, especially with two grandkids close in age, isn't logistics — it's whether to buy two separate gifts or put your budget toward one that works with how their parents are likely already sharing kits between them.

This guide is written specifically for the gift-giver, not the parent — if you're the parent trying to work out the sibling math yourself, our [core cost guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) is the right starting point instead.

## The Feature That Solves Your Biggest Worry

Most gift-giving anxiety around a subscription-based toy comes down to one fear: buying the wrong stage. A grandchild grows fast, and a gift bought in October for a baby who's "about six months old" might arrive, sit unactivated for a few weeks, and then not match where the child actually is by the time it ships.

Lovevery's gifting program is built around exactly this problem. As the gift-giver, you choose how many Play Kits you want to give — not which specific stage. Your grandchild's parent (the recipient) activates the gift whenever they're ready, and at that point, *they* enter the baby's birth date, adjusted birth date, or even a due date if the baby hasn't arrived yet. The system uses that to determine the correct starting box automatically. You're not guessing, and neither are they.

This also means you can buy well ahead of a birthday, holiday, or baby shower without worrying about timing — the gift doesn't expire or lock in a stage at the moment of purchase. It sits ready until the recipient activates it, whenever that ends up being.

## Three Ways to Actually Buy This

There are three distinct purchase paths, and they're not identical — worth knowing which one fits your situation before you buy.

**1. The Play Kits Gift (direct from Lovevery).** Go to lovevery.com, click "Shop," then "Gift Lovevery," then "Gift the Play Kits." You select how many kits you want to give — there are bundle options with savings built in based on quantity, so buying more at once generally costs less per kit than buying a single kit gift and adding another later. The recipient gets a gift code and an activation link, on a date you choose, along with a personal message from you.

**2. The E-Gift Card.** A separate option from the Play Kits gift specifically — this is a flexible dollar-value card usable toward *any* purchase on Lovevery.com, not locked to Play Kits at all. This is the right choice if you're not sure the family wants more Play Kits specifically (maybe they're already well-stocked, or leaning toward a different age range like the Play Gym), and want to let them choose. One limitation worth knowing: Lovevery can't currently attach a personal gift message to e-gift card purchases the way they can with the Play Kits gift, so if the personal note matters to you, the Play Kits gift path handles that better.

**3. Through a baby registry (commonly Babylist).** Lovevery's Play Kits gift bundles are also listed directly on registry platforms — a real, confirmed option if the parents have already set up a registry and you'd rather purchase through that rather than going directly to Lovevery's site. A few registry-specific details worth knowing: bundles here are often sold in multiples of three kits (a "3-Kit Gift" bundle, for example, covers roughly six-plus months at the standard two-month shipping cadence for the 0-12 month tier), and registry purchases typically include a note that two gifted bundles back-to-back cover a full first year. Registry-purchased gifts generally can't ship to Alaska, Hawaii, Canada, or military addresses — worth checking before you buy if your family lives outside the contiguous US.

## The Real Question: One Generous Gift, or Two Separate Ones?

This is the part that's genuinely different for a grandparent buying for two grandkids close in age, and it's worth thinking through rather than just splitting your budget evenly by default.

If the parents are managing two kids under two, there's a real chance they're already leaning on the kind of sibling pass-down math this entire content series is built around — using the older child's outgrown boxes for the younger one rather than running two full subscriptions in parallel. If that's the case, **a generous gift extending the older child's subscription further can end up benefiting both kids**, since a longer active subscription for child one means more material eventually flowing down to child two anyway — without you needing to buy and coordinate two entirely separate gifts.

The alternative — buying two separate, smaller gifts, one addressed to each grandchild — has real appeal too, particularly if you want each child to have something that feels distinctly "theirs" rather than a shared or hand-me-down resource, which matters to some families more than the financial efficiency does. There's no universally right answer here; it depends on how the parents are actually managing things, which is worth a quick check before you commit your budget one way or the other.

**A practical middle ground:** if you're not sure which approach the parents prefer, the e-gift card option covered above sidesteps the question entirely — it lets the parents decide how to allocate the value themselves, whether that's one bigger gift for one child, two smaller ones, or a single standalone item (covered below) for whichever grandchild needs something specific right now.

## Avoiding a Duplicate or Wasted Gift

A few real mechanics worth knowing specifically to avoid overlap with a subscription the parents may already have running:

**If the family already has an active subscription**, a gift can be added directly to it rather than creating a separate, redundant one — the gifted kits apply to whatever's scheduled to ship next on a standard pay-per-kit plan, or immediately following the final kit on a prepaid plan. This is the mechanism that prevents your gift from accidentally duplicating a box the family is already receiving.

**If you want your gift to start at a later kit than the very next one** — for instance, if an older grandchild's hand-me-downs already cover the earliest stages and you'd rather your gift "skip ahead" to a stage the family doesn't already have covered — this isn't self-serve through the standard gifting flow, but per Lovevery's own guidance, it can be arranged by contacting the company directly. Worth doing if you know the family already owns the early-stage items and you don't want your gift dollars going toward something they don't need.

**If a grandchild ages out of a lower age bracket before a multi-kit gift is fully used** (relevant mainly for registry-purchased bundles tied to a specific age range, like the 0-12 month tier), Lovevery has a documented process for converting the remaining value into store credit applicable to later-stage kits — so an unexpectedly fast-growing grandchild doesn't leave part of your gift stranded and unusable.

## Budget-Conscious Alternatives to a Full Gift Subscription

If a multi-kit gift feels like more commitment than you want, or your budget doesn't stretch to two full gifts for two grandkids, a few lower-cost paths are worth knowing about:

**A single standalone item**, rather than a subscription gift. Per our [standalone-buying guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-items-worth-buying-individually), a small number of especially popular individual items — the object permanence box among them — are sold directly, new, for $25-30, without any subscription commitment at all. This is a reasonable, lower-pressure gift if you want to give something specific and well-regarded without navigating the subscription-gifting system.

**A secondhand item through Lovevery's own Pre-Loved marketplace.** Per our [resale guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-pre-loved-resale-listing-guide), buying used through the brand's official resale channel gets you real savings over new pricing while still coming with the platform's own condition grading — a more budget-conscious gift than a full new subscription, without the uncertainty of an anonymous secondhand marketplace purchase.

**A smaller e-gift card amount**, rather than committing to a specific number of full-price kits — this lets the parents stretch your gift toward whatever specific need is most pressing, including a single item rather than a multi-kit commitment.

## A Practical Buying Checklist

1. **Ask (or gently confirm) whether the family already has an active subscription** before buying a separate multi-kit gift — if they do, plan to add your gift to that existing subscription rather than starting a new one.
2. **Decide between one larger gift and two smaller ones** based on how the parents are actually managing the two grandkids, not just an even split by default — a quick conversation is worth it here.
3. **Check your shipping situation** if you're buying through a registry and live outside the contiguous US, or if the grandchildren's family does — registry-purchased gifts have real shipping limitations worth confirming before you buy.
4. **Choose your purchase path** based on how much you want to specify: the Play Kits gift for a targeted, personal gift with a message; the e-gift card for maximum flexibility; a standalone item for something specific and lower-commitment.
5. **If you want your gift to skip ahead of stages the family already has covered**, contact Lovevery directly rather than assuming the standard gift flow handles this automatically.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to know the exact due date or birth date when I purchase the gift, or only when it's activated?**
Only at activation — you don't need this information at the time of purchase at all, which is part of what makes this gifting system genuinely convenient for a grandparent buying ahead of a specific date.

**Can I send the gift to arrive on a specific date, like a birthday?**
Yes — you choose the date the gift notification and code are sent to the recipient, separate from whenever they choose to activate it.

**What if I want to gift something for an older grandchild who's already past the Play Kits age range?**
Lovevery's Play Kits lineup now covers ages 0-4, per our [preschool lineup guide](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-preschool-lineup-past-age-two-second-kid) — a gift purchased for an older grandchild should be selected from the correct age-tier product page, since not every gift bundle (registry-purchased 0-12 month bundles specifically) automatically extends to older age ranges.

**Is it rude to ask the parents directly which purchase option makes the most sense?**
Not at all, and given the real overlap risk with an existing subscription described above, a quick check is genuinely the most practical way to avoid an unwanted duplicate or a gift that doesn't fit how the family is already managing things.

**Does the recipient know the gift came specifically from me, or is it anonymous?**
The Play Kits gift path includes a personal message from you to the recipient, sent alongside the gift code — this is one advantage of that path over the e-gift card option, which currently doesn't support an attached gift message.

**If I buy through a registry and the family later cancels their registry, does my gift still work?**
We don't have specific documented details on this exact scenario — if you have any concern about it, purchasing directly through Lovevery's own gifting flow rather than a third-party registry removes that uncertainty entirely.

**Can I combine a Play Kits gift with a Book Bundle or reading-focused gift?**
Per Lovevery's own gifting program structure, Book Bundles can be added to a Play Kits gift — worth considering if reading is a priority for the family, alongside or instead of the core developmental toy kits.

**Is there a cost advantage to buying more kits at once versus gifting one kit now and another later?**
Generally yes — bundle-based savings apply based on how many kits you choose in a single gift purchase, so consolidating into one larger gift (if your budget allows) is typically more cost-efficient than several smaller, separate gift purchases over time.
