**Bottom line up front:** Coterie is priced at or above Kudos — commonly around $90–$105 a month depending on size and channel, against Kudos's roughly $88–$106+ — and markets itself with "clean," "gentle," and "premium" language throughout. But Coterie's own published ingredient breakdown, confirmed directly through its retail product listings, shows its topsheet, backsheet, and leg cuffs are made from polypropylene, polyethylene, and polyester — plastic, at the layer touching the baby's skin — with only 25% of the overall diaper plant-based. Kudos, priced comparably or lower, uses a 100% cotton topsheet specifically engineered to keep plastic off that same skin-contact layer. This isn't a matter of interpretation or marketing spin: it's a direct, confirmed materials difference between two brands charging premium prices for what a shopper skimming both product pages might reasonably assume are equivalently "clean" products. This article exists because, as far as this research could determine, no one has put these two brands' actual ingredient lists side by side and said this plainly.

## The Materials Claim That Doesn't Hold Up

This is the central finding of this article, and it's worth documenting precisely rather than characterizing loosely.

**Coterie's own retail listings, including a detailed Q&A response on its Babylist product page, confirm the following ingredient breakdown directly:** the absorbent core is made of sodium polyacrylate (SAP) and totally chlorine-free (TCF) wood pulp from sustainably managed forests — the same basic SAP-and-pulp architecture The Hello Bello vs. Dyper audit documented for Hello Bello. But critically, **the backsheet is made from polypropylene, polyester, and polyethylene, and the topsheet — the layer that sits directly against the baby's skin — is made of polypropylene.** A supplementary product description confirms the diaper overall is "made with 25% plant-based materials," meaning roughly three-quarters of the product, by the company's own published figure, is not plant-based. Coterie's wipes, separately, are confirmed as 100% plant-derived and plastic-free — a genuine distinction worth crediting, since it means the plastic content is concentrated in the diaper specifically, not the full product line.

**Kudos, as The Kudos cotton-premium breakdown documented in detail, uses a 100% cotton topsheet** — marketed under the proprietary name TruCotton — specifically engineered so that plastic does not touch the baby's skin at that layer, backed by OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certification. This is a direct, confirmable, structural difference between the two brands' core skin-contact material, not a matter of marketing tone: Kudos's topsheet is cotton: Coterie's topsheet is polypropylene, by each company's own published specification.

**Why this matters for a shopper choosing between them:** Coterie's marketing language — "clean," "gentle on baby's skin," "cashmere-like softness," "apparel-grade materials" — is not inaccurate on its own terms (softness and gentleness are real, plausible outcomes of thoughtful nonwoven engineering, independent of whether the fiber is plant-based or synthetic), but it does not, on the evidence gathered for this research, mean what a shopper comparing it against Kudos's explicit "cotton, not plastic, touches your baby's skin" pitch might reasonably infer. A household paying Coterie's premium specifically because they believe they're avoiding plastic at the skin-contact layer, the same reason a household might choose Kudos, is not getting that specific outcome from Coterie based on the company's own published ingredient list.

## A Caveat Worth Naming: The "Only Cotton Brand" Claim May Already Be Dated

The Honest vs. Kudos comparison, drawing on a gimmethegoodstuff.org review, described Kudos as "currently the only brand using cotton" at the topsheet layer among major clean-ingredient competitors. Trade press coverage found during this research (Nonwovens Industry, June 2025) describes a newer entrant, Cottonsie, launched in May 2025, using 100% cotton across the topsheet, backsheet, acquisition distribution layer, and core wrap — a more extensive cotton application than even Kudos's topsheet-only approach, per that trade coverage. Cottonsie is outside this cluster's scope and wasn't independently verified beyond this one trade-press mention, but it's worth flagging directly: Kudos's cotton-topsheet differentiation, verified as accurate relative to Coterie in this article, may not remain unique in the broader market for long, if it still is today.

## The Absorbency Claims Rematch — and a Real Evidentiary Asymmetry

Coterie's own marketing, confirmed across multiple retail listings reviewed for this research, makes specific, quantified performance claims: **up to 70% more liquid capacity than other leading brands, absorption up to 4x faster, skin kept up to 3x drier, over 16 oz of liquid capacity (based on a Size 4 diaper), and up to 12-hour leak protection.** These are specific, confident figures — but as far as this research could determine, they are brand-published claims, not figures attributed to an independent testing lab, unlike the specific comparison The Kudos 2026 manufacturing investigation and the Honest vs. Kudos comparison documented for Kudos.

Kudos's pre-2026 absorbency advantage over Pampers Pure Protection, Huggies Special Delivery, and Honest Company's diaper was independently tested by Diaper Testing International and reported by NBC News in August 2024 — a real, named third-party testing organization, not a figure Kudos generated internally. **No equivalent independent testing citation for Coterie's specific 70%/4x/3x figures was located in this research.** This is a meaningful evidentiary asymmetry worth naming plainly: Kudos's headline performance claim (as of 2024, before the redesign complications The Kudos 2026 manufacturing investigation documented) came with a named independent tester behind it; Coterie's headline performance claims, based on everything gathered for this article, do not currently have an equivalent independent citation attached to them in the sources this research could locate.

**This doesn't mean Coterie's claims are false** — a company can have accurate internal testing data it simply hasn't had independently replicated or hasn't publicized the source of. But it does mean a shopper weighing "70% more capacity" against Kudos's NBC-reported, independently-tested results is not comparing two claims on equal evidentiary footing, and this article isn't going to present them as though they were.

## The 2026 Kudos Wildcard

The Kudos 2026 manufacturing investigation documented a reported 2026 manufacturing and quality shift affecting Kudos, including reviewer accounts describing thinner, narrower, "scratchy" material and core material becoming visible during heavy use — changes that, if representative, would directly undercut the specific 2024 absorbency advantage referenced above. This matters directly for this comparison: **the independent testing that gives Kudos its strongest evidentiary edge over Coterie's unverified claims was conducted on the pre-2026 product.** If a household is choosing Kudos over Coterie specifically because of that 2024 testing result, The Kudos 2026 manufacturing investigation's findings are directly relevant and should be weighed before assuming that advantage still holds for the diaper currently shipping. This is precisely the kind of cross-cluster connection this whole series is built to surface — a comparison that would look decisively one-sided using only 2024 data looks considerably murkier once the 2026 findings are factored in.

## Real Reviewer Sentiment: Both Brands Show Genuine Polarization

Independent review accounts for both brands, found during this research, show real, substantial disagreement among actual users — worth reporting honestly rather than picking whichever reviews support a cleaner narrative.

For Coterie: one reviewer, having tried five premium brands (Pura, Kudos, Joonya, Bambo Naturals, and cloth diapering via Esembly), ranked Coterie **worst of the five**, describing it as containing "a lot of plastic" (a subjective impression that, per this article's materials-breakdown findings above, has real factual grounding) and reporting it caused "the worst diaper rashes" for their baby. Other reviewers describe the opposite experience: "simply put the best on the market: super soft, amazing absorption, great fit," and a separate account calling Coterie's subscription "costly but easy to manage, pause and cancel," with particular praise for the wipes.

For Kudos: The Kudos 2026 manufacturing investigation documented the same kind of split, concentrated around the March 2026 redesign specifically — strongly positive reviews before that date, a specific and consistent complaint pattern after it.

**The pattern worth drawing from both: neither brand's review history supports a simple "one is good, one is bad" verdict.** Both show a meaningful split between highly satisfied and genuinely dissatisfied users, and for Kudos specifically, that split appears to track a datable product change rather than random individual variation — a distinction this research could not confirm or rule out for Coterie's polarized reviews, since no equivalent before/after pattern by date was identified in Coterie's review history during this research.

## Pricing Rematch

Pricing for both brands showed real variation across the retail channels reviewed for this research, consistent with the channel-pricing variability this cluster has documented for every brand covered so far. Coterie's monthly-supply listing on Babylist prices a 198-diaper Size 1 supply (six packs of 33) at $105, working out to roughly $0.53 per diaper — while other listings referenced a "starting at $90" figure, and one detailed comparison source cited $90/month as Coterie's standard bundle price. Kudos's pricing, per The Kudos cotton-premium breakdown and the Honest vs. Kudos comparison, has shown similar variation, from roughly $88 at direct-subscription channels up to $106+ at certain retail listings.

**The honest read: at realistic, everyday pricing, these two brands sit close enough together — both in the $0.42–$0.63 per-diaper range depending on size and channel — that pricing alone is not a reliable tiebreaker between them.** The materials and evidentiary differences documented above are the more decision-relevant factors for a household choosing between these two specific brands, not the price gap, which is smaller and noisier than either brand's "premium" positioning might suggest.

One structural note worth flagging: the specific $105 Babylist listing referenced above is a one-time "monthly supply" purchase, not an ongoing subscription — Coterie's own direct-to-consumer subscription is a separate mechanism, described favorably by at least one reviewer as "easy to manage, pause and cancel," consistent with the self-service subscription patterns this cluster has documented as increasingly standard across the category.

## Applying This Comparison to a Multi-Child Household

**Both premium brands compared in this article, then, share a real structural disadvantage relative to Hello Bello and Honest for a close-age-gap household spanning more than one size tier apart:** neither Coterie's narrow newborn-stage bundle nor Kudos's single-size subscription structure (per The Kudos cotton-premium breakdown) offers the broad multi-size flexibility The Diaper Bundle Dashboard guide established as a meaningful cost and logistics advantage. A household prioritizing that flexibility specifically, independent of the materials questions this article raises, has more evidence-backed reason to default to Hello Bello or Honest than to either of the two brands compared here.

Yes — Kudos's 100% cotton topsheet is a confirmed, direct contrast to Coterie's polypropylene topsheet, based on both companies' own published materials specifications.
