Finding out you're pregnant again while still nursing your toddler brings a whirlwind of emotions. Alongside the joy of growing your family comes immediate questions: *Is it safe? What happens to my milk supply? How will I handle nursing aversion?* Breastfeeding through pregnancy is entirely possible and practiced worldwide, but it comes with unique physical and mental hurdles — and whether to keep going, start weaning now, or let it resolve on its own is a genuinely personal decision with no one universal answer.

Below we break down what to expect trimester by trimester, how to manage your changing body, and how to prepare for tandem nursing.

:::section
safety-section|Is Breastfeeding Safe During Pregnancy?
badge:🛡️ Medical Safety
badge:🩺 Low-Risk Pregnancies
For most women with uncomplicated, low-risk pregnancies, nursing is entirely safe. When you nurse, your body releases small amounts of oxytocin, which can cause mild uterine contractions. However, in a healthy pregnancy, these contractions do not induce preterm labor — and many people continue through to delivery and beyond, into [tandem nursing once the new baby arrives](/blog/feeding-schedule-two-different-ages).
- **Consult if you have:** A history of preterm labor, incompetent cervix, or unexplained vaginal bleeding.
- **Consult if needed:** Carrying multiples (twins or triplets).
- **Follow specific guidance:** If your OB-GYN advises pelvic rest or abstaining from uterine contractions due to complications.
:::

:::warning
**Worth Knowing:** "Most" isn't "all" — always verify your specific medical standing with your OB-GYN or midwife during your first prenatal visit if you plan to continue nursing. This is a conversation for your provider specifically, not something to decide from general information alone.
:::

:::section
supply-section|What Happens to Your Milk Supply?
badge:🍼 Milk Supply & Colostrum
badge:🔄 Weaning vs. Continuing
Hormones are powerful architects. Around 16 to 20 weeks gestation, your hormonal profile shifts dramatically, causing your milk supply to drop significantly. Furthermore, your milk transitions into nutrient-rich colostrum as your body prepares for the new baby — a normal physiological response, not a sign you're doing anything wrong.
- **Dry Nursing:** Many toddlers continue nursing purely for comfort and closeness even when milk volume drops to near zero.
- **Self-Weaning:** Some toddlers dislike the salty taste of colostrum or the reduced flow and decide to self-wean entirely during the second trimester — independent of any decision you make.
- **The Surge:** Once your milk comes in fully postpartum, your toddler may suddenly find themselves back in business with a high-flow refill.
:::

:::warning
**Pro Tip:** Do not panic if your toddler tugs and fusses because milk flow has slowed down. Offer extra verbal reassurance and cuddles to bridge the gap during supply dips. Abrupt weaning is generally harder on both of you than a gradual transition if you have the flexibility to choose.
:::

:::section
aversion-section|Navigating Nursing Aversion (BAA)
badge:🧠 Mental Health
badge:⚠️ Breastfeeding Aversion (BAA)
One of the least talked about — yet most common — aspects of nursing while pregnant is **Breastfeeding Aversion and Agitation (BAA)**. Hormonal shifts can trigger a sudden, intense wave of irritation, rage, or skin-crawling discomfort the exact moment your toddler latches on. Fatigue compounds in a way that's hard to overstate, so plan for [more rest and support](/blog/solo-bedtime-2-under-2-alone) than either situation would require by itself.
- **Set Strict Time Limits:** Use a timer or count to 20, then gently unlatch your toddler.
- **Distraction Techniques:** Have your toddler nurse while looking at a book or singing a song together to redirect your focus.
- **Give Yourself Permission to Wean:** If aversion becomes overwhelming for your mental health, gentle weaning is a loving choice for both you and your toddler — and it's [emotionally easier](/blog/one-on-one-time-each-kid-close-in-age) than an abrupt stop.
:::

## If you're deciding whether to wean now

This is a genuinely personal decision with no version of "what's right" that doesn't depend on specifics only you and your provider know — your milk supply, your toddler's relationship with nursing, any pregnancy complications, your own physical and emotional capacity.

**Abrupt weaning is generally harder on both of you than gradual weaning**, if you have the flexibility to choose. A slower transition tends to be more comfortable physically (reducing risk of engorgement) and emotionally easier for a toddler.

**A lactation consultant can help regardless of which direction you're headed** — whether you want support continuing through pregnancy and into tandem nursing, or support weaning gently. This is specifically their area of expertise.

**There's no deadline you're failing to meet.** Whether you nursed for two months or two years, whether you wean before the new baby or nurse both afterward, none of these timelines are a measure of anything except what worked for your family.

## If you decide to continue, or to tandem nurse after the new baby arrives

Many people do this successfully, and resources specifically about tandem nursing go into far more practical detail than fits here — worth seeking out specifically if this is the direction you're headed, since the logistics (positioning two kids of very different sizes, managing supply for both) are genuinely their own topic. :::checklist
📋 Pregnancy & Nursing Survival Checklist
- **Confirm With Your Provider:** Clear continued breastfeeding with your OB-GYN or midwife at your first scan.
- **Boost Calories & Hydration:** Increase your daily caloric intake and hydration to support both baby and toddler.
- **Prioritize Rest:** Growing a baby while producing colostrum is intensely demanding on your energy reserves.
- **Set Firm Boundaries:** Establish firm boundaries around nursing sessions if skin sensitivity or aversion flares up.
- **Prepare for Either Outcome:** Get mentally ready for your toddler to self-wean or happily transition to tandem nursing.
:::
