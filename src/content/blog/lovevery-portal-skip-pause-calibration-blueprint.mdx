# Exploiting the Lovevery Portal: The Advanced Step-by-Step Blueprint to Skipping, Pausing, and Calibrating Shipments for Close-Age Siblings

**Bottom line up front:** Lovevery's subscription platform gives you three real levers — skip a box, delay a ship date, or cancel and reactivate — and no true "pause" function. Used well, those three levers can save a close-age-sibling household several hundred dollars across the first two years without ever missing a developmentally necessary box. Used carelessly, they can leave a younger sibling without stage-appropriate material for months. This guide walks through the mechanics in full, then builds out calibrated timelines for three specific gap scenarios so you're not guessing.

This is the operational companion to our [core sibling cost math](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-subscription-worth-it-two-kids-under-two) and the [box-by-box durability audit](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) — both explain *why* pass-down works; this one explains exactly *how* to time your subscription around it.

## The Click-by-Click Portal Walkthrough

Lovevery's account management for Play Kits runs through the website, not exclusively the app, and it's worth knowing the exact path before you're trying to figure it out during a sleep-deprived 11 p.m. scroll.

**To skip an upcoming box (the tool you'll use most):**
1. Log in at lovevery.com — this specific setting is not available inside the mobile app.
2. Navigate to **Account Settings**.
3. Locate **Manage Subscription** and select the skip option for the specific box you want to skip.
4. Confirm the skip at least 3 days before your next scheduled ship date. Requests submitted after that window will not apply in time and the box will ship as normal.

**To adjust a shipping date without skipping entirely:**
Use the same Account Settings > Manage Subscription path, but select the date-adjustment option instead of the skip option. This pushes a specific box later without removing it from your subscription sequence.

**To manage shipments from your phone:**
1. Open the Lovevery app.
2. Go to the **My Lovevery** tab.
3. Tap **Manage My Play Kits** to view and adjust upcoming shipments from a mobile-friendly interface.

**To cancel and reactivate (the tool for longer breaks):**
There's no dedicated "pause" button on the standard Play Kits subscription — per Lovevery's own support documentation, achieving an extended break means either using the skip tool repeatedly (one box at a time, each requiring its own confirmation) or cancelling the subscription outright and reactivating later, at which point your next box will match your child's stage at the time you reactivate, not where you left off.

**A critical caveat for Prepay & Save subscribers:** if you're on an annual prepaid plan rather than paying box-by-box, skipping a specific kit is not self-serve — you'll need to contact customer support directly to process it. This matters enormously for the calibration strategies below, because a prepaid plan removes a lot of the flexibility those strategies depend on. If you're planning to run a staggered, sibling-timed schedule, a standard pay-per-box plan gives you far more real-time control than a prepaid one, even though the prepaid plan is nominally cheaper per box.

**On perks and access:** skipping a box does not affect your subscriber status, your pricing tier, or your access to the parent app content and guides — you retain full account access between skipped boxes. The one exception, noted above, is promotional pricing tied to consecutive shipments on certain signup offers; check your original terms if you locked in an introductory rate before skipping several boxes in a row.

## The Age-Gap Calibration Matrix

Below are three full calibration timelines, built for the three most common close-age-sibling scenarios. Each assumes Child A is born at month 0 and Child B is born at the stated gap. Adjust to your own exact dates, but the skip logic holds within a few weeks of these windows.

### 10-Month Gap Calibration

| Timeframe (from Child A's birth) | Child A's Stage Focus | Child B's Stage Focus | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Months 0-9 | Early baby boxes | — | Run normally | Building the base collection before sibling #2 arrives |
| Month 10 | Approaching toddler transition | Newborn | Do not start a new subscription for Child B yet | Child A's earliest boxes are barely worn and still correct for a newborn |
| Months 10-13 | Toddler-transition boxes | 0-3 months | Skip Child A's next 1-2 scheduled boxes | Household doesn't need two active subscriptions running simultaneously yet |
| Months 13-14 | Resume normal toddler boxes | 3-4 months | Resume Child A's subscription at current stage | Child A has aged past what Child B is currently using |
| Month 15 | Toddler boxes continue | 5 months | Start Child B's own subscription | Child B now needs finer-motor material Child A's hand-me-downs don't cover |
| Months 15-22 | Toddler boxes continue | 5-12 months | Run both, skip either child's box if it clearly duplicates something the other still has in active rotation | Ongoing overlap in broad-use items continues reducing total boxes needed |

Estimated savings over the first 15 months: roughly 1-2 skipped boxes. Because Child A is transitioning from the $80/box, 0-12 month tier into the $120/box, 13-24 month tier right around this window, the actual dollar value depends on which side of that transition the skipped box falls on — roughly $80-$200.

### 14-Month Gap Calibration

| Timeframe (from Child A's birth) | Child A's Stage Focus | Child B's Stage Focus | Action | Rationale |

By this point Child A is already past 12 months and into the $120/box tier, so this single skip is worth roughly $120 — a narrower dollar figure than the 10-month gap's range, but with meaningfully less manual tracking required, since the timing naturally does more of the work.

An 18-month gap family gets most of their savings just by storing things well and doesn't need to manage shipments nearly as aggressively.

## Managing a Second Subscription Line: Book Bundles and Early Reader Club

Some families layer a second Lovevery product on top of the core Play Kits — the Book Bundles or an Early Reader Club subscription for ages 0-4. These run on entirely separate schedules with their own skip rules, and conflating them with your Play Kits calibration is a common mistake.

For the Early Reader Club specifically, skipping or rescheduling an upcoming box follows a similar path to Play Kits: log in, go to Account Settings, scroll to Manage Reading Subscription, and select Modify/Skip on the relevant subscription. One meaningful difference: once the Early Reading Club (ages 0-4) transitions into the Reading Skill Set boxes for ages 4+, skipping stops being available entirely, because those later activities are sequential and build directly on each other — at that point your only lever is pausing or rescheduling the next shipment by up to 30 days, not skipping it outright.

If your household is running both a Play Kits subscription and a reading-focused subscription for two kids close in age, treat them as two entirely separate calibration problems. A skip on one line has zero effect on the other, and the "line them up so both kids overlap" logic from the Play Kits calibration tables above doesn't map cleanly onto the reading subscription's different cadence and stricter later-stage skip restrictions.

## Troubleshooting Common Calibration Problems

**Problem: I skipped a box, but now my older child seems ahead of or behind where the box would have put them.**
This usually means the skip window ran longer than the child's actual developmental pace justified. The calibration tables above are starting points based on typical stage timing — if your specific child is developing faster or slower than the average window, adjust your resume date to match your child's actual observed skills rather than sticking rigidly to the month-based table.

**Problem: I'm on a Prepay & Save plan and support hasn't responded to my skip request in time.**
Because prepay skips aren't self-serve, timing risk is real — submit your request well before the 3-day cutoff, not at the deadline, to leave room for a support delay. If a box ships before your request is processed, most households simply treat it as an early delivery of a stage-appropriate box rather than a wasted purchase, since the box will still match your child's current stage.

**Problem: My two kids are fighting over an item that's supposed to be "passed down," not shared simultaneously.**
This is a sign your gap has entered one of the higher-friction windows identified in our [box-by-box audit](https://www.siblingstack.com/blog/lovevery-two-under-two-box-by-box-audit) — certain items (blocks, ball runs, ramps) generate real simultaneous demand rather than clean sequential handoff. At that point, the calibration schedule's savings estimate should be adjusted downward, since you may end up purchasing a supplemental piece rather than relying on pure pass-down for that specific item.

**Problem: I started my calibration schedule late — my second child is already several months old and I didn't plan ahead.**
Join the relevant timeline at whatever point matches your current situation rather than treating the missed early months as unrecoverable. You won't get back savings from months already passed, but the overlap-based savings in the middle and later parts of each table still apply starting from today.

## The "Missing & Broken Pieces" Policy Deep Dive

This matters directly to the calibration math above, because every skip-and-resume cycle assumes the items you're relying on for the gap period are actually intact.

Per Lovevery's own support documentation, replacement part requests require proof of purchase — from Lovevery.com directly, the official Pre-Loved resale marketplace, or an authorized retailer such as Amazon or Target. Requests lacking that proof generally aren't supported, which is worth knowing if you received a hand-me-down box from a friend or bought one secondhand through an unofficial channel rather than Pre-Loved specifically.

In practice, real parent reviews on this point vary meaningfully. Several describe receiving free replacement pieces after a single support conversation — one European reviewer specifically noted losing a small piece and having Lovevery ship a replacement at no cost. Others describe a slower, more effort-intensive process, particularly involving live chat support that some reviewers characterize as heavily automated and less responsive for anything beyond a scripted issue. Delivery and shipping-related complaints appear more frequently in review data than replacement-part complaints specifically, suggesting the core replacement process itself functions reasonably well when a request is properly documented, even if the surrounding support experience varies.

**The practical calibration takeaway:** if a piece from a box you're relying on for pass-down goes missing (say, a wooden coin from The Thinker that a rough toddler flings under the couch), don't assume a guaranteed free replacement will arrive in time to matter for your skip schedule. Log the claim as soon as you notice the issue, keep your original proof of purchase accessible, and build a small buffer into your skip timeline rather than assuming full box integrity right up to your planned resume date.

## Pros and Cons of Active Skip Management

**Pros:**
- Meaningful savings during the most expensive early stretch, especially for tighter gaps
- Less clutter from running two full subscriptions simultaneously when one isn't yet needed
- Full per-box control, unlike a blanket pause

**Cons:**
- **Loses the milestone-tracking design** if skipped too aggressively, handing a child material that's behind or ahead of their actual stage
- **Requires manual stage-tracking** for two kids instead of trusting the subscription calendar
- **Prepay plans complicate execution**, requiring a support conversation instead of a self-serve toggle
- **Narrow gaps benefit most**; wide gaps (18+ months) get comparatively little from active skip management and more from simple storage discipline

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I skip more than one box in a row?**
Yes, with no hard limit, as long as each skip is confirmed individually before its 3-day cutoff.

**Will skipping mess up my child's spot in the stage sequence?**
No — skipping doesn't reorder your child's stage; your next box still matches wherever your child currently is developmentally.

**Is it better to skip Child A's boxes or delay starting Child B's subscription?**
For gaps under 15 months specifically, delaying Child B's start date is usually simpler, since it means managing one subscription calendar instead of two during the highest-overlap window.

**Does skipping affect my price or lock-in rate?**
Not on standard pricing. Promotional rates tied to consecutive shipments are the exception — check your signup terms.

**What if I skip a box and then realize my child needed it?**
You can resume on the next scheduled cycle; you generally can't retroactively recover the exact skipped box on the original timeline, but the next kit still matches your child's current stage.

**Will my younger child miss out developmentally by using hand-me-down items instead of their own fresh box?**
Not meaningfully, provided the items are still stage-appropriate for where your younger child actually is — the developmental value of an object doesn't diminish because a sibling used it first.

**Does the 3-day skip cutoff apply the same way across all three gap calibrations above?**
Yes — the cutoff is a fixed account policy regardless of your specific gap or calibration strategy, so build it into whichever timeline you're following as a hard planning constraint.

**If I'm on a Prepay & Save plan, should I switch to pay-per-box before attempting one of these calibration timelines?**
For tighter gaps (10-14 months) where active skip management does the most work, switching to pay-per-box before your sibling-timed schedule begins gives you meaningfully more self-serve flexibility. For an 18-month-plus gap, where the calibration table above shows minimal skip activity anyway, the prepay discount may be worth keeping since you won't be leaning on the skip tool much regardless.

**How do I know which specific box my older child is currently on without logging into the account?**
You don't need to guess — your account's Manage Subscription page displays your child's current stage and next scheduled ship date directly, which is the same page you'd use to execute any of the skip or delay actions above.
