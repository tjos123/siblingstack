**Bottom line up front:** All three major subscription brands — Hello Bello, The Honest Company, and Dyper — let you swap some or all of a diaper shipment for training pants without cancelling or restarting your subscription, but none of them hold your per-unit value constant when you do it. That's not a hidden fee or a gotcha — it's a straightforward function of training pants being a more complex, higher-cost-to-manufacture product than a standard diaper — but it's a real financial shift worth planning for before your toddler starts asking for "big kid underwear," not after you notice your usual bundle is running out faster than it used to.

## The Honest Company: A Fully Worked, Verifiable Example

Honest's subscription structure is the easiest of the three to calculate precisely, because the brand publishes both diaper-pack and training-pant-pack counts, and because subscribers can swap pack-for-pack within the same flat $79.95–$89.99 bundle (pricing has varied across the years we reviewed sources from, as noted in [the Diaper Bundle Dashboard guide](/blog/diaper-bundle-portal-mixing-sizes-blueprint)).

Take a household with a toddler in Honest's Size 5 diapers (25 diapers per pack) transitioning into 3T/4T training pants (19 pants per pack, based on standalone retail listings for that size). If that household swaps all 6 packs in their bundle from Size 5 diapers to 3T/4T training pants:

:::table
 | Diapers (Size 5) | Training Pants (3T/4T) 
 Units per pack | 25 | 19 
 Packs in bundle | 6 | 6 
 Total units delivered | 150 | 114 
 Bundle price | Same flat price | Same flat price 
 Effective cost per unit | ~$0.53 (at $79.95/150) | ~$0.70 (at $79.95/114) 
:::

That's a **24% reduction in total pieces delivered for the identical subscription price** — a real, calculable tipping point, not a marketing estimate. A household that doesn't anticipate this and keeps ordering on the same delivery cadence will find their training-pants bundle running out roughly a week earlier each cycle than their diaper bundle did, unless they proactively shorten the delivery interval or supplement with a-la-carte purchases.

Hello Bello's Combo Bundle (diapers plus training pants in one order, described in [the Diaper Bundle Dashboard guide](/blog/diaper-bundle-portal-mixing-sizes-blueprint)) is the mechanism intended for exactly this transition. What we could not confirm from Hello Bello's own published subscription documentation is the exact per-pack training-pant count included in a Combo Bundle slot, or whether swapping a diaper pack for a training-pants pack inside the standard bundle structure preserves the same 6-to-7-pack framework Honest and Dyper use. ## The Realistic Timeline: When This Transition Actually Happens

Subscription planning for this transition works better with realistic timeline expectations, and the published pediatric and developmental data is consistent enough across sources to be useful here, even though — as with everything in this series — individual children vary widely and none of this should be read as a target to hit rather than a general pattern.

Most children show developmental readiness signs for potty training somewhere between 18 and 24 months, per commonly cited American Academy of Pediatrics guidance, though actual active training more often begins somewhat later in practice — commonly cited data puts the average age of actively starting around 27 months. Daytime training completion clusters around 2 to 3 years old in most sources reviewed, with a commonly cited process length of roughly 3 to 6 months from active start to consistent daytime success. Girls tend to complete daytime training a few months earlier than boys on average, though this is a population-level pattern, not a predictor for any individual child. Nighttime dryness is a distinctly separate and later milestone — commonly not achieved until age 4 or 5, and in some children later still, since it depends on bladder-signal maturity during sleep rather than the same learned behaviors involved in daytime training.

One dataset worth flagging because it directly affects subscription planning: **relapses are common even after a child has been reliably dry for an extended stretch** — one commonly cited figure puts relapse rates as high as 25% among children who had been dry at night for six months or longer, usually tied to a temporary stressor (illness, a household change, a new sibling). For a subscription household, this means the "switch entirely to training pants and cancel the diaper line" instinct is worth resisting in the early months of a successful transition — the Agility Rating Matrix above exists specifically because regressions are a normal, expected part of this process, not an edge case.

**What this means for a narrow-age-gap household specifically:** if your potty-training toddler is on the earlier end of the readiness window (starting around 24 months) while a younger sibling is born into the same household, you're likely managing an active, non-linear training process for one child and full-time diapering for the other simultaneously for a stretch of several months at minimum — the 3-to-6-month daytime training window is the optimistic case, and nighttime training extending well beyond that is the norm rather than the exception. Plan your subscription mix for a multi-month mixed-needs period, not a single clean cutover month.

## Why Training Pants Cost More to Manufacture Than Standard Diapers

The 15–25% pieces-per-dollar reduction documented above isn't arbitrary pricing — it reflects real, verifiable differences in how training pants are constructed compared to a standard tabbed diaper. Training pants are built as a fully closed garment with elasticized sides that a toddler pulls up and down independently, which requires either a seamless circular-knit-style construction or bonded side seams strong enough to withstand repeated independent pull-on/pull-off cycles — a structural requirement standard diapers, which are fastened fresh with adhesive tabs at each change, don't have. This is consistent with why training pants marketed by Honest specifically describe a "contoured fit" and reference the value of a garment-like construction, and why cost-per-unit for training pants runs meaningfully higher than a same-size diaper across every pack-count comparison in this guide. This is general manufacturing and product-design knowledge rather than a brand-specific claim, and it explains the tipping point documented above as a structural product-cost difference rather than a subscription-pricing markup unique to any one brand.

## The "Accident Prevention" Workflow

Potty training regressions are common and expected, not a sign that anything has gone wrong — and the subscription-management side of a regression (getting diapers back in the rotation) is usually the easy part, based on the Agility Rating Matrix above. The harder part is the physical household logistics of running changing tables, diaper pails, and a potty chair simultaneously, especially with a second child still fully diapered in the same household. A few practical layout and workflow adjustments that specifically address the narrow-age-gap scenario:

**Zone your supplies by function, not by child.** Rather than maintaining a full changing setup in each child's room, concentrate diapering and training-pants supplies in one or two fixed zones (typically the changing table location and a downstairs or shared-space bathroom), stocked with both diapers and training pants regardless of which child is using which product that week. This reduces the number of places you need to remember to restock when your subscription's pack mix shifts, and means a regression doesn't require reorganizing a dedicated toddler-only station.

**Keep the potty chair visible and accessible even during a diaper-heavy stretch.** If your subscription mix has shifted back toward diapers after a regression, resist the instinct to put the potty chair away — visibility and continued casual access are commonly recommended by pediatric and child-development sources as supportive of eventual re-engagement with training, without requiring active pressure during the diapered stretch.

**Plan your delivery-date adjustment before the regression, not during it.** Since all three brands allow frequency and content adjustments through account settings (per the Agility Rating Matrix above), the highest-leverage move during an actual regression is going into your account and shifting the next shipment back toward diapers immediately, rather than waiting to see if the regression resolves within a few days. Given standard processing-to-delivery windows (3–7 business days for Hello Bello, roughly similar ranges for Honest and Dyper based on this series' earlier research), a delayed decision here can mean a full week of running short on whichever product you actually need.

**For a two-diapering-plus-one-training household specifically**, the practical answer to "changing table vs. diaper pail vs. potty chair" is usually to keep the youngest child's full diapering setup completely separate from the toilet-training toddler's zone, even if they're physically close together — cross-contaminating the two workflows (using the same wipes-and-cream caddy for both, for instance) is a common source of the exact kind of daily friction and misplaced-supply frustration that makes this transition period feel harder than it needs to be. A single shared "on-deck" restock zone (the closet or shelf where new packs live before they're distributed to the two smaller working zones) is a reasonable middle ground that avoids duplicating your entire supply chain twice while still keeping the two children's day-to-day changing routines separate.

**Adjust wipes allocation deliberately.** Because Hello Bello and Honest both include a fixed 4-pack wipes allotment regardless of what's happening on the diaper/pants side of the bundle, a household with one child in training (who typically needs fewer, quicker wipe uses per change) and one child still fully diapered doesn't need to rebalance wipes the way they need to rebalance diapers-versus-pants — this is one part of the transition that doesn't require active subscription management, which is worth knowing so you're not solving a problem that doesn't exist.

Pulling together the timeline data and the tipping-point math above, here's what a realistic multi-month budget looks like for a single toddler moving through this transition, using Honest's verified pack-count figures as the worked example (since it's the brand with the most complete published data across both products).

Assume a toddler begins active daytime training at the commonly cited average age of around 27 months, takes the commonly cited average of 4 months to reach daytime consistency, and — consistent with the nighttime-dryness data above — continues needing overnight coverage for a year or more beyond that.

:::table
 Month | Likely Mix | Approx. Units Needed | Subscription Approach 
 Month 1–2 (active daytime training, high accident rate) | Mostly diapers, training pants for outings only | Full diaper-bundle volume | Keep standard diaper bundle; no subscription change needed yet 
 Month 3–4 (daytime training solidifying) | Shift toward training pants for daytime, diapers for naps/overnight | Mixed — roughly half diapers, half pants | Swap 2–3 of 6 packs to training pants (Honest's pack-by-pack model)

The practical takeaway repeated throughout this series holds here too: re-check your bundle configuration at least monthly during an active transition, rather than the quarterly cadence that's reasonable once both children have settled into stable, predictable consumption patterns.

- **If you want the most granular control over a household straddling diapers and training pants simultaneously**, Honest's pack-by-pack swap mechanism is the best-documented fit among the three brands covered here, based on available information.

- **Regardless of brand**, budget for a 15–25% reduction in pieces-per-dollar once training pants enter the mix, and treat any regression as a signal to adjust your account settings immediately rather than waiting it out — the lag between decision and delivery is real across all three services.

**Does switching to training pants cost more per unit than diapers?**
Based on real pack-count data from Honest (24% fewer pieces for the same price) and Dyper (17% fewer pieces for the same price), yes — the flat bundle or box price stays the same, but you receive meaningfully fewer pieces once training pants replace diapers in the mix.

**Can I switch back to diapers if my toddler regresses after starting training pants?**
Yes, for all three brands — this is a standard account-settings adjustment for your next shipment, not a subscription restart, based on available documentation.
