# Best Convertible Car Seats for Two Kids Close in Age (2026)

*Seats worth buying twice, and what to know before you do.*

When two kids are close in age, you're often buying two car seats within a year or two of each other. That makes "will this actually last?" and "will two of them fit in my back seat?" much bigger questions than they are for a one-child family. Below are four convertible seats that come up repeatedly in independent testing, with the real numbers, the trade-offs, and what to check before you buy.

> **Read this first:** Don't take a car seat's safety on a blog post's word, including this one. Before buying, check the seat's recall status at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), read the manufacturer's manual, and confirm the seat installs correctly in *your* vehicle. Installation problems are a more common real-world issue than the seats themselves. Treat this page as a starting point for research, not a substitute for it.

## Maxi-Cosi Pria All-in-1: longest usable lifespan

**Who it's for:** Families who want to buy as few seats as possible.

The Pria is a 3-in-1: rear-facing, forward-facing with a harness, then a belt-positioning booster up to 100 lb and about 52 inches. One seat per child can plausibly carry you from the hospital through early elementary school. It's also certified for airplane use in rear-facing and forward-facing harness modes, and the cover is machine-washable.

**Good to know:** The QuickFit headrest and harness adjust together as your child grows. BabyGearLab found the rear-facing installation awkward, since the seat had to be angled far back, which made it top-heavy. Try it in your car if you can.

**Pros**

- Covers every stage in one seat
- Machine-washable cover
- Airplane-certified

**Cons**

- Rear-facing install can be fiddly
- Rear-facing limit is 40 lb, lower than the Britax

## Chicco Fit360 ClearTex: easiest to load, but not a long-life seat

**Who it's for:** Parents who are tired of leaning into the back seat, and who accept buying a booster later.

The Fit360 rotates 360 degrees with one hand, so you can turn the seat toward the door to load a child, then rotate it to face the right way for the ride. Visual ready-to-ride indicators show when it's locked in position. A second base is available if you want to move the seat between two cars.

**Good to know:** The Fit360 has no booster mode. It's a harness-only seat, rear-facing to 40 lb and forward-facing to 65 lb, so you'll need a separate booster later. It's also heavy, at roughly 31 to 34 lb, and it costs noticeably more than the others. It makes loading easier, and the indicators help you confirm a secure install, but you should still follow the manual and have the installation checked if you're unsure.

**Pros**

- Rotation makes loading and unloading far easier
- Ready-to-ride indicators
- Second base available for another vehicle

**Cons**

- No booster mode
- Heavy and the most expensive of the four

## Britax Poplar: extended rear-facing

**Who it's for:** Families who want to keep a child rear-facing as long as possible.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends keeping children rear-facing as long as possible, up to the limits of the seat. The Poplar's rear-facing limit of 50 lb is higher than the 40 lb limit on the other three seats here, and it has a steel-reinforced frame. Forward-facing harness use goes to 65 lb.

**Good to know:** Britax sells both the Poplar and the Poplar S, and the S is the roughly 17-inch-wide version. Check which model you're buying and its dimensions. Like the Chicco, it has no booster mode.

**Pros**

- Highest rear-facing weight limit of the four
- Steel-reinforced frame

**Cons**

- No booster mode
- Wider than the Graco

## Graco SlimFit3 LX: best for tight back seats

**Who it's for:** Families squeezing two or three seats into one row, or anyone watching their budget.

At 16.7 inches wide, the SlimFit3 LX is one of the narrowest full-range seats available. It rear-faces to 40 lb, harnesses forward-facing to 65 lb, then converts to a high-back booster from 40 to 100 lb (the booster stage is for ages 4 and up). It has a built-in seat-belt lock-off for forward-facing installs.

**Good to know:** Width is a screening tool, not a guarantee. Belt geometry, seat contours, and front-seat clearance all decide whether three seats really fit. Graco itself notes the cup holders can't be used with the anti-rebound bar installed for rear-facing.

**Pros**

- Narrow, with a booster mode
- Belt lock-off for forward-facing

**Cons**

- Rear-facing limit is 40 lb

## Buying two seats at once: what to check

- **Test the fit before you commit.** Manufacturer "fits three across" claims are optimistic more often than not. Put both seats in your actual back seat side by side before removing tags or packaging. See [our guide to choosing car seats for two very differently sized kids](/reviews/gear/car-seat-two-different-sizes) for more.
- **Match installation systems if you swap cars.** If both seats use similar latching, moving them between vehicles or reinstalling after cleaning is far less of a chore.
- **Rear-facing limits matter most for the younger child.** Choose seats whose rear-facing limits match how long you want to keep each child rear-facing.
- **Buy for the booster stage, too.** If you want to avoid buying a separate booster later, an all-in-one seat like the Pria or SlimFit3 LX may save money overall.

## When to switch stages

Move a child out of a mode only when they reach that mode's weight *or* height limit, not just because of age. Rear-facing is safest for as long as the seat allows. For boosters, check the age minimum on the manual as well as weight and height. Most of these seats also have an expiry date, usually printed on the seat and around 10 years from manufacture, and a seat should be replaced after a moderate or severe crash.

## Buyer's checklist for two convertible seats

- Check NHTSA recall status for every seat you're considering.
- Measure your back seat width and note where the belts and LATCH anchors sit.
- Confirm the rear-facing and forward-facing limits match your timeline for each child.
- Check that both seats use compatible latching if you swap them between vehicles.
- Test side-by-side fit before you throw away packaging.
- Read the manual, and consider having a certified child passenger safety technician check your install.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which convertible car seat lasts the longest?

Of these four, the Maxi-Cosi Pria All-in-1 and the Graco SlimFit3 LX last longest, because both convert to a booster that goes up to 100 lb. The Chicco Fit360 and Britax Poplar are harness-only, so you'd buy a booster later.

### Which convertible car seats fit three across?

The Graco SlimFit3 LX is one of the narrowest at 16.7 inches, and Graco says it fits three across. Actual fit depends on your vehicle and the other seats, so test before you buy.

### How long should my child stay rear-facing?

The AAP recommends rear-facing as long as possible, up to the seat's weight or height limit. The Britax Poplar rear-faces to 50 lb, while the other three here go to 40 lb.

### Is a rotating car seat safer?

Rotation mainly makes loading and unloading easier. Safety depends on a correct install and correct use, so follow the manual either way.

### Do convertible car seats expire?

Yes. Most carry an expiry date, often around 10 years from manufacture, printed on the seat. Check yours.
