**Bottom line up front:** You don't choose your daycare's app — the program does, and it's free to you regardless of which one they've picked. The good news buried in that fact: if both of your children's programs happen to use the same platform (Brightwheel, specifically, has built this well), one parent login can unify both children's daily reports, photos, and billing under a single account, even across two entirely different centers. The bad news, and the reason this comparison exists: if your infant's daycare uses one platform and your toddler's preschool uses a different one — a genuinely common outcome in a small-age-gap household, since infant care and toddler/preschool care are often provided by different types of programs — there is no bridge between them. You'll be checking two separate apps, with two separate logins, and no shared view of your two children's days.

## Why You Don't Get to Pick, and What That Actually Means

Daycare and preschool management platforms are business-to-business software: the childcare program pays a monthly fee (typically ranging from roughly $15–$30 as a base fee plus $2–$3 per enrolled child on Brightwheel's Premium tier, per published third-party pricing estimates, since none of these companies publish exact rates publicly) to run attendance tracking, billing, staff scheduling, and — the part that actually matters to you — parent communication. The parent-facing app is free specifically because the revenue model charges the institution, not the family using it. This means your only real point of leverage as a parent is which program you enroll your child in, not which software that program happens to run — and most families don't ask about app platform when touring a daycare, reasonably prioritizing safety, ratios, and curriculum over software preference.

## The Genuinely Good News: Same-Platform Sibling Unification

This is worth highlighting because it's a real, well-built feature that doesn't get much attention: Brightwheel's own documentation confirms that a parent contact can be linked to multiple student profiles across different programs using the same login — including profiles at entirely separate centers, not just siblings at the same location. If your infant is in a Brightwheel-run in-home daycare and your toddler is in a Brightwheel-run preschool across town, one account, one login, and one app genuinely does show you both children's information, without needing two separate sign-ups or a workaround. Switching between the children's individual profiles within that one account is the only additional step required.

This is a meaningfully better cross-institution model than several other systems covered elsewhere in this cluster — it beats Huckleberry's shared-credential approach (see our full review) specifically because it's built around individual, portable parent identity rather than a single shared household login, and it's a genuine point in Brightwheel's favor if you have any influence over which programs your children attend and both could plausibly land on the same platform.

## The Real Problem: Two Kids, Two Platforms, No Bridge

The unification above only works if both programs use the same underlying software. If your infant's daycare runs on Brightwheel and your toddler's preschool runs on Lillio, there is no cross-platform account, no shared login, and no unified feed — full stop. This is not a limitation either company is likely to fix, since they're direct competitors with no incentive to build interoperability with each other's systems. For a small-age-gap household specifically, this scenario is common rather than an edge case: infant care slots are frequently scarcer and more expensive than toddler/preschool slots in many markets, pushing families toward whatever infant program has availability — which may have no relationship to, and no shared vendor with, wherever the older sibling already attends.

### Practical Coping Strategies

- **Check both apps on a fixed schedule rather than reactively.** Since there's no unified notification feed, deciding on set check-in times (end of workday, before dinner) for each app separately reduces the mental overhead of monitoring two separate notification streams throughout the day.
- **Use each platform's own export or report feature for pediatrician visits**, rather than trying to manually reconcile two systems' data into one document — most platforms in this category, including Brightwheel and Lillio, offer some form of daily/weekly report export a parent can save or print, though the exact format and depth varies by platform and by what your specific program has enabled.
- **If a nanny, grandparent, or co-parent also needs visibility into both apps**, treat this as effectively two separate instances of the caregiver-access question covered in [our permission models guide](/reviews/digital-services/caregiver-access-permission-models) — each platform's own sharing/invite mechanism needs to be set up independently, since there's no single point of control spanning both.
- **If you do have some choice in child care providers** (for instance, choosing between two comparably good preschool options for your toddler while your infant is already settled at a specific in-home daycare), asking each prospective program which platform they use — and prioritizing a match with your other child's existing platform, all else being roughly equal — is a legitimate, if minor, factor worth adding to the decision, purely for your own day-to-day convenience rather than anything related to program quality.

## What This Doesn't Solve

It's worth being honest about the limits of even the best-case scenario here. Same-platform unification solves the login and viewing-access problem, but it doesn't merge your two children's data into a single combined view within the app — you're still looking at two separate child profiles, just from inside one account rather than two. None of these platforms are designed to answer a question like "compare my infant's and toddler's nap schedules side by side," the way a purpose-built multi-child tracker like Huckleberry or Pebbi is — daycare communication software is built around one child's day at a time, not comparative household-level insight across children, even when both children are enrolled in the same underlying system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**If my toddler's preschool switches from Tadpoles to Brightwheel mid-year, do I need to create a new account?**
Per Brightwheel's own documentation, if a child moves to a new program that also uses Brightwheel, your existing account can be linked to the new profile without creating a new one — but if the switch is from a different platform entirely (like Tadpoles) to Brightwheel, you'll need to set up a new Brightwheel account for that transition, since accounts aren't portable between different companies' software.

**Can I request that my infant's daycare and my toddler's preschool both switch to the same platform for my convenience?**
This is unlikely to be something a single family's preference would change, since a childcare program's software choice is typically an institution-wide decision tied to their own operations, billing, and staff training — it's a reasonable thing to mention if you have a good relationship with a program's director, but not something to expect will happen on your account alone.

**Does any platform in this category let me compare my two children's schedules or reports side by side, even if they're on the same platform?**
Not in the way a dedicated baby-tracking app does — daycare communication platforms are built around one child's individual daily report, not comparative multi-child analytics, even when a parent's account includes both children.

**Is there a cost to me as a parent for using any of these apps, regardless of which one my daycare picked?**
No — in every case covered here, the childcare program pays for the software, and the parent-facing app is free to use regardless of platform.

**If I have children at two different centers that both use Brightwheel, will staff at each center be able to see both of my children's information?**
No — Brightwheel's contact system is scoped so that staff and contacts only see the students they're specifically assigned to; your unified parent login lets you view both your own children's profiles, but it doesn't give either center's staff visibility into the other center's students or your other child's information.

**Do these apps integrate with baby-tracking apps like Huckleberry or Pebbi, so I don't have to enter the same information twice?**
Not that's documented across the platforms compared here — daycare communication software and personal baby-tracking apps are built by separate companies without integration between them, so information logged by daycare staff (naps, feeds, diaper changes during the day) and information you log yourself at home generally exist as separate, non-synced records.

**Should the daycare app a program uses factor into which daycare I choose in the first place?**
For most families, no — safety, staff ratios, curriculum, and location should reasonably take priority over which communication software a program happens to use; the app is a genuine day-to-day convenience factor, but a minor one relative to the actual quality and fit of the care itself.

**If my child's daycare doesn't use any of these platforms and instead uses paper daily reports, is that a red flag?**
Not necessarily — some smaller or specifically philosophy-driven programs (Montessori, in-home providers, etc.) intentionally use lower-tech communication methods, and this isn't inherently a quality signal in either direction; it simply means you won't have the kind of real-time digital updates this comparison covers, and any pediatrician-visit documentation would need to come from your own home logging instead.
